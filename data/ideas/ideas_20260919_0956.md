## 1. FEE SCOUT NG  

**PROBLEM** – Nigerians looking to send or receive money abroad cannot quickly see which provider (Western Union, MoneyGram, Wise, local banks, fintechs) offers the lowest total fee for a given amount and destination. *(Google‑Trends: “money transfer fees” – 500 + monthly searches)*  

**SOLUTION** – A lightweight Android app + web portal where users enter amount, origin, destination, and receive a side‑by‑side list of providers, total cost, estimated delivery time, and a “send now” affiliate link. The backend scrapes public fee tables (or uses provider APIs) and caches results on AWS Free‑Tier (Lambda + DynamoDB).  

**WHO PAYS** –  
* Primary: Nigerians (age 18‑45) who regularly remit money to family abroad (≈ 2 M potential users).  
* Secondary: Remittance providers that pay per‑lead (affiliate).  

**PRICE** – Free to end‑user. Revenue via **affiliate commissions** (≈ ₦200‑₦500 per successful transfer) plus an optional **Premium “no‑ads + price‑alert”** tier at **₦500 / month**.  

**EXISTING COMPETITORS** – Wise (formerly TransferWise), Remitly, WorldRemit, local bank calculators, informal WhatsApp groups. None are a **mobile‑first, Nigeria‑specific, fee‑only comparator**.  

**MONETIZATION FIT** – Remittance providers already spend heavily on performance‑based affiliate programmes; users are accustomed to free comparison tools but will tolerate a modest ad‑free premium if it adds price‑alerts.  

**FIRST WEEK** –  
1. **Data‑gathering sprint** – write a Python scraper for the top 5 providers (use free tier Lambda).  
2. **MVP UI** – build a single‑screen Android activity (input fields + list) and a matching React‑lite web page.  
3. **Affiliate sign‑ups** – contact Wise, Remitly, Western Union for affiliate links; embed them in the results page.  

**WHY NOW** – The “money transfer fees” search trend is stable, and recent devaluation of the Naira has heightened fee sensitivity. No Nigerian‑centric comparator exists yet, creating a clear first‑mover edge.

## 2. HAU‑SA‑FACT  

**PROBLEM** – Nigerian internet users searching “**hausa legit**” are trying to verify the authenticity of Hausa‑language news, social‑media posts, or product claims, but no dedicated fact‑checking tool exists in that language. *(Google‑Trends: “hausa legit” – 200 + monthly)*  

**SOLUTION** – A mobile/web app where users paste a Hausa text or URL; the service runs a lightweight NLP pipeline (open‑source Hausa language models + a small custom classifier) to flag potential misinformation, provide source credibility scores, and optionally generate a “✅ Legit” or “⚠️ Dubious” badge that can be shared. The backend runs on AWS Free‑Tier (Lambda + S3) using HuggingFace’s free inference API for the language model.  

**WHO PAYS** –  
* Primary: Hausa‑speaking social‑media users (≈ 15 M potential).  
* Secondary: Local media outlets & NGOs that need bulk verification (B2B).  

**PRICE** – Freemium: 5 checks / day free; **₦300 / month** for unlimited checks + API access for organisations.  

**EXISTING COMPETITORS** – International fact‑checkers (AFP, BBC) – not Hausa‑specific; community‑run WhatsApp verification groups (informal). No dedicated SaaS.  

**MONETIZATION FIT** – Individual users are price‑sensitive but may pay for unlimited checks if they see value; NGOs and media are more likely to purchase a modest subscription for credibility.  

**FIRST WEEK** –  
1. **Collect a labelled Hausa dataset** (scrape verified news vs. known hoaxes).  
2. **Fine‑tune a small transformer** (e.g., distilBERT‑hausa) on free GPU (Google Colab).  
3. **Build a simple Android front‑end** that calls a Lambda‑hosted inference endpoint.  

**WHY NOW** – The “hausa legit” search term signals a fresh, unmet demand; recent political events have increased misinformation in regional languages, creating urgency.

## 3. CAMPUS‑WEATHER  

**PROBLEM** – University students in Nigeria search “**weather forecast today**” and “**map weather**” but generic apps don’t give campus‑specific alerts (e.g., flooding of a particular dorm, heat‑wave warnings for outdoor labs). *(Google‑Trends: both terms ≥ 100 monthly)*  

**SOLUTION** – An Android app that pulls open‑source weather data (OpenWeatherMap free tier) and overlays it on a **campus map** (uploaded by the university). Users can subscribe to **geo‑fenced push alerts** (e.g., “Rain expected at University of Lagos, Faculty of Law”). The backend stores campus polygons in DynamoDB; Lambda computes alerts and pushes via Firebase Cloud Messaging (free tier).  

**WHO PAYS** –  
* Primary: Students (age 16‑25) at large Nigerian universities (≈ 1 M potential).  
* Secondary: University administrations that want to broadcast safety alerts (B2B).  

**PRICE** – Free for students. **₦2 000 / year** per university for an admin dashboard + branded alerts.  

**EXISTING COMPETITORS** – General weather apps (AccuWeather, Weather.com), campus‑specific SMS alert systems (often manual). No app combines real‑time weather with campus GIS data.  

**MONETIZATION FIT** – Universities have modest IT budgets and are willing to pay for a safety‑focused service; students expect free weather info, so a B2B model is realistic.  

**FIRST WEEK** –  
1. **Partner with one university** (e.g., University of Ibadan) to obtain campus GIS data (publicly available).  
2. **Set up OpenWeatherMap free API** and Lambda function to query and store forecasts.  
3. **Release a minimal Android client** that shows current weather + a “Subscribe to campus alerts” toggle.  

**WHY NOW** – Climate‑related disruptions (floods, heat) have risen in Nigeria; universities are under pressure to improve student safety, creating a timely niche.

## 4. TUNNELBOX  

**PROBLEM** – Developers (especially students) want to expose a local development server to the internet for demos, but Cloudflare’s **Quick Tunnels** require CLI knowledge and manual token handling. *(Hacker News discussion on “Cloudflare Quick Tunnels”)*  

**SOLUTION** – A simple Android/web UI that authenticates the user’s Cloudflare account (OAuth), lets them select a local port, and spins up a tunnel with a single tap. The service runs a thin Lambda proxy that forwards traffic; the Android app shows the public URL and logs.  

**WHO PAYS** –  
* Primary: Student developers & hobbyists (age 18‑30) who need quick demos.  
* Secondary: Small startups that run occasional hack‑day demos.  

**PRICE** – Free tier: up to **2 tunnels** per month. **₦1 000 / month** for unlimited tunnels + custom sub‑domains.  

**EXISTING COMPETITORS** – Ngrok (free tier limited, paid plans), LocalTunnel (self‑hosted), Cloudflare’s own CLI (no UI).  

**MONETIZATION FIT** – The market is price‑sensitive; many already use free Ngrok. However, a **no‑code UI** could justify a modest subscription for power‑users and small teams.  

**FIRST WEEK** –  
1. **Register a Cloudflare account** and obtain API credentials for testing.  
2. **Prototype the tunnel‑creation Lambda** (reuse Cloudflare’s public API).  
3. **Build an Android front‑end** with a single “Start Tunnel” button and QR‑code display of the public URL.  

**WHY NOW** – Remote‑learning and hack‑athons have surged post‑COVID; students need frictionless ways to showcase projects. The lack of a UI for Cloudflare tunnels is a clear gap.

## 5. STUDY‑BUDGET  

**PROBLEM** – Nigerian students struggle to track tuition fees, transport costs, and installment‑payment options; they also face hidden fees on money‑transfer services used to pay school fees. *(GitHub issues: “Money Transfer Fees”, “Support for monthly installments on credit‑card transactions”, “Template Transactions”)*
  
**SOLUTION** – A personal‑finance Android app tailored to students that:  
1. Lets users input recurring expenses (tuition, rent, transport).  
2. Pulls live fee data from the **FeeScout NG** service for any money‑transfer they plan to use.  
3. Suggests the cheapest installment plan (e.g., “pay tuition in 3 × ₦5 000 via Paystack”).  
4. Generates a monthly “budget‑vs‑actual” report.  

**WHO PAYS** –  
* Primary: University students (≈ 1 M) who need budgeting help.  
* Secondary: Fintech partners (Paystack, Flutterwave) that pay for leads.  

**PRICE** – Free core budgeting; **₦400 / month** for “Premium” (fee‑comparison integration + installment‑plan suggestions).  

**EXISTING COMPETITORS** – General budgeting apps (Money Guru, PocketGuard) – not Nigeria‑specific; no fee‑comparison integration.  

**MONETIZATION FIT** – Students are highly price‑sensitive, but a **premium** that demonstrably saves them money on tuition/fees can convert. Fintech affiliate revenue adds a secondary stream.  

**FIRST WEEK** –  
1. **Integrate FeeScout NG API** (reuse the code from Idea 1).  
2. **Design a simple expense‑tracker UI** (Android + local SQLite).  
3. **Launch a landing page** (via the marketer) targeting university Facebook groups to collect early sign‑ups.
