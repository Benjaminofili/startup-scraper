## 1. RideGuard NG  

### 1. Problem  
Riders repeatedly complain that Uber‑type apps charge opaque “cancellation fees” and let drivers cancel at the last minute, leaving users over‑charged and stranded. *(Play‑Store complaints excerpts)*  

### 2. Solution  
A **lightweight Android app + web dashboard** that:  

* tracks every ride request made on the major local platforms (Uber, Bolt, Gokada) via their public APIs or screen‑scraping (within T&C limits),  
* records the quoted fare, any cancellation fee, and driver‑cancel events,  
* shows a **real‑time “fair‑price” benchmark** based on historic data,  
* lets users **file a one‑click complaint** that is automatically forwarded to the platform’s support channel, and  
* builds a community‑rated “trust score” for drivers and for each platform.  

Revenue comes from **premium analytics** sold to the platforms (they love data on churn) and a **small “tip” fee** (₦200‑₦500) for users who want the complaint to be escalated as a “priority” case.

### 3. Who Pays  
* **Power users** (daily commuters, ride‑hailing freelancers) who need price certainty – they’ll pay the tip or a monthly “Pro” subscription.  
* **Ride‑hailing companies** (B2B) that want anonymised churn & fee‑dispute data to improve their pricing engine.

### 4. Price  
* Free tier – basic tracking & community scores.  
* Pro subscription – **₦1 200 / month** (≈ $2.50) for unlimited premium analytics & priority complaint handling.  
* B2B data package – **₦30 000 / month** for aggregated, anonymised reports (negotiable).

### 5. Existing Competitors  
* **Bolt & Uber** – have in‑app support but no transparent fee‑benchmarking.  
* **Gokada** – similar.  
* **No dedicated third‑party watchdog** in Nigeria (globally there are “RideCheck”‑type services, but none local).  

### 6. Monetization Fit  
* **Riders** are price‑sensitive but willing to pay a few hundred naira for a guarantee of “no surprise fees”.  
* **Companies** already spend on data analytics; a small, niche data set is attractive if it can demonstrably reduce churn.  

### 7. First‑Week Launch Steps  
1. **Validate API access** – register as a developer on Uber, Bolt, Gokada; test fetching ride‑quote data for a single city (Lagos).  
2. **Build a minimal MVP** – Android screen that lets a user paste a ride‑request URL and shows the quoted fare + “cancellation‑fee risk” (simple rule‑based).  
3. **Run a micro‑campaign** – post the MVP in Lagos‑based commuter Facebook groups, collect 20‑30 beta users, and ask for their “pain‑point” stories (to refine the value prop).  

### 8. Why Now  
* Recent surge of complaints on the Play Store shows the problem is **top‑of‑mind**.  
* Ride‑hailing market in Nigeria is still **fragmented**; platforms are eager for data to retain users.  

### 9. Feasibility Scores  

| Metric | Score (0‑10) |
|--------|--------------|
| **Investment** | **9** – uses free AWS tier, existing Play Store account, no hardware. |
| **Passive Income** | **5** – B2B data can be automated; consumer tip fees need manual monitoring initially. |
| **Team Size** | **8** – 1 Android dev, 1 backend/API dev, 1 marketer; no need for extra hires. |
| **Time to Market** | **7** – MVP possible in 2‑3 weeks; full data pipeline 1‑2 months. |
| **Competition** | **6** – No direct competitor, but ride‑hailing giants dominate the user experience. |
| **Monetization Fit** | **5** – Users are price‑sensitive; B2B side is stronger. |
| **Regulatory Risk** | **8** – No licensing needed, but must respect each platform’s T&C (scraping risk). |

## 2. UniAccess NG  

### 1. Problem  
Students searching “unilag” are looking for **online‑distance‑learning (ODL) content** that is scattered, outdated, or behind university portals that are hard to navigate.  

### 2. Solution  
A **mobile‑first web portal** that aggregates:  

* Official lecture recordings, PDFs, and assignment briefs (pulled via public university APIs or by partnering with student societies).  
* A **personalised timetable** that syncs with Google Calendar.  
* Peer‑to‑peer Q&A (similar to StackOverflow) **tagged by course code**.  

Revenue: **monthly student subscription** for “premium” content (high‑quality PDFs, offline download) and **institutional licences** for departments that want a white‑label version.

### 3. Who Pays  
* **Undergraduate & postgraduate students** (especially those in ODL programmes).  
* **University departments** that want a branded, ad‑free portal for their students.

### 4. Price  
* Free tier – basic timetable & community Q&A.  
* Premium student – **₦1 500 / month** (≈ $3) for full‑downloadable resources & ad‑free experience.  
* Institutional licence – **₦150 000 / semester** (≈ $300) for a custom domain & analytics dashboard.

### 5. Existing Competitors  
* **UNILAG’s own portal** – fragmented, often down.  
* **Google Classroom / Moodle** – used but not curated for Nigerian curricula.  
* **WhatsApp study groups** – informal, no centralised content.  

### 6. Monetization Fit  
* Nigerian students **do pay** for exam prep and private tutoring; a modest monthly fee for reliable, consolidated ODL material is realistic.  
* Institutions have modest budgets for digital tools; a white‑label licence is plausible if the product demonstrably improves student outcomes.

### 7. First‑Week Launch Steps  
1. **Contact the UNILAG ODL office** – request permission to republish publicly available lecture links (or at least to embed them).  
2. **Scrape the first 2‑3 courses** (e.g., Computer Science 101) and build a simple React web page with a timetable widget.  
3. **Run a pilot** – share the link in the university’s official Facebook page and collect sign‑ups (target 50‑100 students).  

### 8. Why Now  
* Google‑Trends shows a **spike in “unilag” searches** (likely driven by new ODL intakes).  
* Existing university portals are **over‑loaded** and students are turning to ad‑hoc solutions (WhatsApp groups).  

### 9. Feasibility Scores  

| Metric | Score |
|--------|-------|
| **Investment** | **9** – free AWS, open‑source web stack, no hardware. |
| **Passive Income** | **6** – subscription renewals can be automated; content updates need occasional manual work. |
| **Team Size** | **8** – 1 web dev, 1 Android dev (optional), 1 marketer. |
| **Time to Market** | **6** – MVP (one course) in 2 weeks; scaling to full catalogue 2‑3 months. |
| **Competition** | **5** – University portal exists but is poor; informal groups are not “competitors” in the SaaS sense. |
| **Monetization Fit** | **7** – Students in Nigeria already spend on study aids. |
| **Regulatory Risk** | **9** – Only copyright issues if you host copyrighted material without permission; can mitigate by linking only. |

## 3. LegalAid Connect  

### 1. Problem  
“Criminal defense attorney” receives **200 + monthly searches** in Nigeria, indicating a sizable need for affordable, accessible legal counsel that is not met by traditional law‑firm phone trees.  

### 2. Solution  
A **two‑sided marketplace** (web + Android) where:  

* **Clients** submit a brief case description, upload documents, and are matched (algorithmically & manually) with vetted criminal‑defense lawyers.  
* **Lawyers** set their own hourly or per‑consultation rates, receive leads, and conduct video/voice calls through the app (using Twilio free tier or WebRTC).  

Revenue: **platform fee** (15 % of each paid session) and optional **subscription** for lawyers to get premium lead placement.

### 3. Who Pays  
* **Defendants / families** who need a quick, affordable initial consultation (often low‑income).  
* **Law firms / solo practitioners** seeking a steady flow of leads.

### 4. Price  
* Client – **₦2 000** for a 30‑minute video consult (pay‑as‑you‑go).  
* Lawyer – **15 %** of the session fee (platform cut).  
* Optional lawyer subscription – **₦5 000 / month** for “top‑of‑feed” placement.

### 5. Existing Competitors  
* **LawPadi**, **LegalZoom Nigeria** – focus on civil matters, not criminal defence.  
* **WhatsApp lawyer groups** – informal, no escrow.  
* **Traditional law‑firm hotlines** – expensive, not app‑based.  

### 6. Monetization Fit  
* Legal services are **high‑value** and clients are accustomed to paying per‑consultation.  
* However, **price‑sensitivity** is high for low‑income defendants; the platform must keep fees low and transparent.  

### 7. First‑Week Launch Steps  
1. **Recruit 3‑5 vetted criminal‑defense lawyers** (via personal network or bar association) willing to pilot the platform for free.  
2. **Build a simple “lead capture” web form** that stores case details and notifies the lawyer via email/SMS.  
3. **Create a landing page** (using free AWS S3 + CloudFront) targeting “criminal defense attorney” search traffic with Google Ads (small budget – can start with ₦0 by leveraging organic SEO).  

### 8. Why Now  
* Search data shows a **steady demand** for criminal‑defense counsel.  
* Mobile penetration in Nigeria is > 80 %, yet **no dedicated legal‑consult marketplace** exists for this niche.  

### 9. Feasibility Scores  

| Metric | Score |
|--------|-------|
| **Investment** | **8** – only needs free AWS, Twilio free tier, no physical assets. |
| **Passive Income** | **4** – each session requires lawyer time; platform fee is automated but revenue is transaction‑based. |
| **Team Size** | **7** – 1 backend/API dev, 1 Android dev, 1 marketer; lawyers handle the service side. |
| **Time to Market** | **5** – legal compliance (terms, privacy) and vetting lawyers take ~1 month. |
| **Competition** | **6** – few direct competitors, but bar associations could view it as “unauthorised practice”. |
| **Monetization Fit** | **7** – legal clients do pay for advice; lawyers pay for leads. |
| **Regulatory Risk** | **4** – must ensure compliance with Nigerian Bar Association rules, data protection (NDPR), and possibly escrow handling. |

## 4. FootyFlash NG  

### 1. Problem  
Fans repeatedly search for “UEFA champions league highlights”, “worldcup 2026”, “celtics” etc., but existing free sources (YouTube, TikTok) are **data‑heavy**, have **ads**, and are often blocked or throttled on cheap mobile data plans.  

### 2. Solution  
A **lightweight Android app** that:  

* Curates **15‑second to 2‑minute highlight reels** from official league feeds (via public APIs or partner agreements).  
* Optimises video bitrate for **2G/3G** (≤ 500 KB per clip).  
* Allows **offline pre‑download** of a day’s highlights for a small fee.  
* Generates **revenue via non‑intrusive native ads** (e.g., small banner) and a **premium ad‑free tier**.

### 3. Who Pays  
* **Football fans** (15‑35 y) who want quick, data‑light highlights.  
* **Advertisers** targeting a male‑skewed, sports‑interested audience.

### 4. Price  
* Free tier – ad‑supported, 5 highlights per day.  
* Premium – **₦500 / month** (ad‑free, unlimited downloads, higher‑quality video).  

### 5. Existing Competitors  
* **YouTube**, **TikTok**, **Facebook Watch** – free but data‑heavy, ad‑overloaded.  
* **Goal.com app**, **ESPN** – some highlight clips but often geo‑blocked or subscription‑only.  
* **Local sports blogs** – no dedicated mobile app.

* Sports fans **spend on subscriptions** (e.g., Showmax, DSTV). A low‑cost, data‑friendly alternative can attract a niche willing to pay ₦500/month.
