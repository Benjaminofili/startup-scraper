### 1. **RideTrust NG**

**PROBLEM** – Riders on Uber/Bolt complain of long wait times, hidden price surcharges and drivers who “don’t accept rides” (Play‑Store reviews).  

**SOLUTION** – A lightweight Android/web overlay that (a) shows the *estimated* price from the primary app *plus* a transparent “service fee”, (b) displays a driver‑trust score (based on crowd‑sourced ratings, vehicle photos, licence verification), and (c) when the primary app fails to match a driver within 5 min, automatically routes the request to a vetted “fallback” driver pool (the team’s own small driver network).  

**WHO PAYS** – Urban commuters (18‑45) who use ride‑hailing at least twice a week and are willing to pay a small premium for reliability; also B2B “corporate travel” accounts that need SLA guarantees.  

**PRICE** – ₦300 / ride (added on top of the normal fare) *or* corporate subscription ₦15 000 / month for unlimited “trust‑layer” access and SLA reporting.  

**EXISTING COMPETITORS** – Uber/Bolt themselves (no trust overlay), **SafeRide** (South‑African driver‑rating add‑on, not available in Nigeria), **Gojek** (Indonesia) – none operate a third‑party trust layer in Nigeria.  

**MONETIZATION FIT** – Nigerian riders are price‑sensitive but have shown willingness to pay for “guaranteed ride” services (e.g., premium UberX). Corporate travel budgets already allocate for reliability tools, so the B2B angle is realistic.  

**FIRST WEEK**  
1. Scrape public Uber/Bolt APIs (or use reverse‑engineered endpoints) to fetch price estimates and driver‑status in Lagos.  
2. Build a minimal Android UI that overlays the price + a “Trust Score” badge; seed the driver database with 20 manually‑verified drivers (friends/family).  
3. Launch a closed‑beta on the Play Store, recruit 50 riders via university WhatsApp groups, collect feedback & iterate.  

**WHY NOW** – The complaints are fresh (2024‑2025) and ride‑hailing penetration in Nigeria is still growing; no local competitor is addressing reliability, creating a clear gap.  

**FEASIBILITY SCORES**  
- Investment: **9/10** (all tech can run on AWS free tier; driver pool is human‑cost only)  
- Passive Income: **5/10** (needs ongoing driver vetting & dispute handling)  
- Team Size: **8/10** (3 devs can handle API, Android UI, backend)  
- Time to Market: **7/10** (MVP in 2‑3 weeks)  
- Competition: **6/10** (dominant ride‑hailing apps exist, but no direct overlay)  
- Monetization Fit: **7/10** (premium riders & corporates do spend)  
- Regulatory Risk: **8/10** (no licensing, just a service layer; must avoid claiming to be a transport operator)

### 2. **BizChat NG**

**PROBLEM** – Small Nigerian businesses (e‑commerce, local stores) find AI chatbots “impossible to use” and often get generic, English‑only responses that don’t understand local slang (Play‑Store review).  

**SOLUTION** – A SaaS chatbot that plugs into WhatsApp Business, Instagram DM and a simple web widget. It uses a lightweight LLM fine‑tuned on Nigerian English & Pidgin corpora (open‑source models hosted on AWS free tier) and offers pre‑built “order‑status”, “FAQ”, and “appointment‑booking” flows.  

**WHO PAYS** – Micro‑SMEs (1‑10 employees) that receive >50 messages/day on WhatsApp; also freelancers offering services (beauty, tutoring).  

**PRICE** – ₦1 000 / month for up to 1 000 messages, ₦3 000 / month for 5 000 messages, plus a 5 % transaction fee on sales closed via the bot.  

**EXISTING COMPETITORS** – **ChatGPT API** (costly per token), **ManyChat**, **BotStar**, **WhatsApp Business API** (requires approved provider, expensive). None are locally‑trained for Nigerian dialects or priced for micro‑SMEs.  

**MONETIZATION FIT** – Nigerian micro‑SMEs spend on advertising (Facebook ads) and are accustomed to paying for lead‑generation tools; however, they are very price‑sensitive, so low‑tier pricing must be compelling.  

**FIRST WEEK**  
1. Pull a small open‑source LLM (e.g., Llama‑2 7B) onto the free‑tier EC2 instance; fine‑tune on a scraped dataset of Nigerian WhatsApp conversations (publicly available).  
2. Build a simple webhook that connects the model to WhatsApp Business Cloud API (free for up to 1 000 messages).  
3. Reach out to 10 local shop owners (via university alumni network) and offer a free 2‑week pilot; collect usage data.  

**WHY NOW** – AI hype is at a peak, but the “one‑size‑fits‑all” bots are failing in Nigeria; a locally‑tuned, cheap solution meets a clear, documented frustration.  

**FEASIBILITY SCORES**  
- Investment: **8/10** (free tier can host a 7B model with quantisation; fine‑tuning cost is zero)  
- Passive Income: **6/10** (once the bot is live, revenue is recurring, but occasional model updates needed)  
- Team Size: **7/10** (requires NLP expertise, but 3 devs can split API, model, front‑end)  
- Time to Market: **6/10** (MVP in 3‑4 weeks due to model fine‑tuning)  
- Competition: **5/10** (global bots exist, but none localized; still a competitive market)  
- Monetization Fit: **6/10** (SMEs will pay if ROI is clear)  
- Regulatory Risk: **9/10** (no licensing, just data handling – must respect GDPR‑like rules, but low risk)

### 3. **ReadEarn NG**

**PROBLEM** – Nigerian students and lifelong learners lack incentives to read regularly; existing platforms are either free but ad‑heavy or paid with high subscription fees.  

**SOLUTION** – An Android app that aggregates open‑access textbooks, news articles, and short‑form educational videos. Every 5 minutes of reading earns “ReadCoins”. Coins can be redeemed for mobile data bundles (via local telco APIs) or for premium content (exam‑prep PDFs). The app gamifies streaks, leaderboards and offers “micro‑tasks” (e.g., summarise an article) for extra coins.  

**WHO PAYS** – Secondary‑school students (15‑19) and university undergrads who need extra study material; parents who purchase data bundles for their kids.  

**PRICE** – Free download; optional “Boost Pack” ₦500 / month for 2× coin earnings and ad‑free experience. Data‑bundle redemption is at cost (telco partnership).  

**EXISTING COMPETITORS** – **Khan Academy** (global, no local data bundles), **Tuteria** (tutoring marketplace), **Google Play Books** (paid textbooks), **Local PDF sharing groups on WhatsApp** (informal). No dedicated “read‑to‑earn” platform in Nigeria.  

**MONETIZATION FIT** – Nigerian youths are accustomed to “play‑to‑earn” models (mobile games) and often purchase data bundles; converting reading time into data is a compelling value proposition. However, the market is price‑sensitive; the premium tier must offer clear extra value.  

**FIRST WEEK**  
1. Curate a library of 200 open‑access PDFs (NCERT, MIT‑OpenCourseWare) and host them on S3 (free tier).  
2. Implement a timer‑based coin system in the Android app; integrate a simple leaderboard using Firebase (free).  
3. Partner with a local telco’s USSD API (many offer sandbox access) to allow users to redeem coins for 50 MB data bundles.  

**WHY NOW** – The “micropayment for reading” experiment in Singapore shows the model works elsewhere; Nigeria’s mobile‑data market is exploding, and there’s a clear gap for an education‑focused incentive app.  

**FEASIBILITY SCORES**  
- Investment: **9/10** (all content is open‑access; AWS free tier covers hosting)  
- Passive Income: **5/10** (requires ongoing content curation and telco partnership)  
- Team Size: **9/10** (solo‑friendly; 1 dev can build the Android app, another handles backend)  
- Time to Market: **8/10** (MVP in 2 weeks)  
- Competition: **7/10** (no direct competitor, but many free content sources)  
- Monetization Fit: **6/10** (students will spend on data; premium tier may be modest)  
- Regulatory Risk: **9/10** (no licensing, just copyright‑cleared content)

### 4. **ScorePulse NG**

**PROBLEM** – Nigerians love live football scores but existing apps are data‑heavy, filled with ads, or blocked on low‑end phones; users also want a community to discuss predictions.  

**SOLUTION** – A lightweight Android app (≈2 MB) that streams only the essential JSON feed for Nigerian league, Premier League, La Liga, etc. It includes a simple “predict‑the‑score” game where users stake “PulsePoints” (earned daily). Winners get a share of a small prize pool funded by ads or a ₦200 / month premium tier (ad‑free + deeper stats).  

**WHO PAYS** – Football fans (13‑45) who follow multiple leagues; advertisers targeting a highly engaged male demographic; small‑scale betting enthusiasts who like low‑stakes prediction games.  

**PRICE** – Free with interstitial ads; Premium: ₦500 / month (ad‑free, advanced stats, higher prize pool).  

**EXISTING COMPETITORS** – **LiveScore**, **SofaScore**, **FlashScore** (global, data‑heavy, sometimes blocked), **Nigerian sports blogs** (manual updates). No low‑data, gamified Nigerian‑focused app exists.  

**MONETIZATION FIT** – Nigerian football fans are accustomed to paying for premium sports content (e.g., DStv). However, they are also ad‑tolerant; a modest premium tier is realistic, but heavy reliance on ad revenue may be volatile.  

**FIRST WEEK**  
1. Subscribe to a free football‑data API (e.g., API‑Football free tier) and set up a Lambda function to cache only needed fields.  
2. Build the Android UI with a minimal list view and a simple prediction modal; store user points in DynamoDB (free tier).  
3. Release a closed beta to university sports societies; gather feedback on latency and prize‑pool mechanics.  

**WHY NOW** – Google‑Trends shows “live scores today” and “la liga table” are hot searches; the market is hungry for a low‑data, locally‑branded experience.  

**FEASIBILITY SCORES**  
- Investment: **9/10** (free API tier, AWS free tier)  
- Passive Income: **6/10** (ad revenue is semi‑passive; prize‑pool management needs oversight)  
- Team Size: **8/10** (2 devs can handle API + Android, 1 marketer for community)  
- Time to Market: **8/10** (MVP in 10 days)  
- Competition: **5/10** (global score apps dominate, but none low‑data local)  
- Monetization Fit: **5/10** (ad‑based revenue plausible; premium conversion modest)  
- Regulatory Risk: **7/10** (no gambling license needed if stakes are “points”, not cash)
