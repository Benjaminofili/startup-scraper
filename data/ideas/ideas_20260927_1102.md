## 1. RidePulse – Real‑time reliability & price‑comparison for ride‑hailing

| **Item** | **Details** |
|---|---|
| **PROBLEM** | Nigerian riders complain that Uber‑type apps “often fail to provide rides when needed”, show “different price for me”, and have “very bad driver support” (Play‑Store review dump). |
| **SOLUTION** | A lightweight Android + web app that (a) pulls live ETA & price quotes from the public APIs of Uber, Bolt, and any local mini‑apps (via reverse‑engineered endpoints), (b) scores each driver‑pool on historical acceptance‑rate & cancellation‑rate (crowd‑sourced from users), and (c) lets the rider pick the most reliable/cheapest option. The app also logs “failed rides” and can auto‑file a complaint to the provider. |
| **WHO PAYS** | Urban commuters (18‑35 y) who use ride‑hailing at least 2 × week and are willing to pay for a “no‑surprise” experience. |
| **PRICE** | ₦500 / month subscription (≈ $1) for unlimited price‑compare + reliability score. A free tier shows only the cheapest price (no reliability data). |
| **EXISTING COMPETITORS** | • Uber & Bolt apps (no cross‑provider comparison)  <br>• “Bolt‑price‑compare” Chrome extensions (very niche, not mobile)  <br>• Local “price‑watch” Telegram channels (manual, no API) |
| **MONETIZATION FIT** | Nigerian riders are price‑sensitive **but** a small monthly fee for guaranteed reliability is attractive when they have repeatedly lost money/time. However, conversion from free to paid is historically low (< 5 %). |
| **FIRST WEEK** | 1️⃣ Scrape publicly documented price‑estimate endpoints for Uber & Bolt (they already exist for “fare‑estimate”).  <br>2️⃣ Build a simple Android UI that shows the two quotes side‑by‑side.  <br>3️⃣ Launch a closed‑beta on the university’s WhatsApp group, collect screenshots & acceptance‑rate data for the first 50 rides. |
| **WHY NOW** | The Play‑Store reviews show a *spike* in dissatisfaction (multiple recent 1‑star complaints).  Uber/Bolt have not yet released a native “compare” feature, and users are actively looking for alternatives. |
| **FEASIBILITY SCORES** | **Investment:** 9/10 (only free‑tier AWS + existing APIs)  <br>**Passive Income:** 4/10 (needs subscription management & support)  <br>**Team Size:** 8/10 (solo‑friendly core, marketer for acquisition)  <br>**Time‑to‑Market:** 7/10 (MVP in ~2 weeks)  <br>**Competition:** 5/10 (strong incumbents, but no direct comparator)  <br>**Monetization Fit:** 5/10 (price‑sensitive market)  <br>**Regulatory Risk:** 9/10 (no licensing, just data aggregation) |
| **OVERALL SCORE (average)** | **6.4** |

## 2. EduGuard – AI‑Generated Assignment Detector for Nigerian Universities

| **Item** | **Details** |
|---|---|
| **PROBLEM** | “How I changed teaching after AI managed to do all my homework assignments” (HN post) – lecturers are losing control of assessment integrity. |
| **SOLUTION** | A web‑service + Android “upload‑assignment” client that runs a lightweight transformer‑based detector (e.g., Open‑Source **GPT‑Zero** model) against submitted text, returns a confidence score, and highlights likely AI‑generated passages. The API can be integrated into university LMS (Moodle, Canvas) or used by individual lecturers. |
| **WHO PAYS** | University departments, private tutoring centres, and individual lecturers who need to protect exam integrity. |
| **PRICE** | ₦2 000 / month per department (≈ $4) for up to 5 000 analyses; ₦500 / month for individual lecturers (≤ 500 analyses). |
| **EXISTING COMPETITORS** | • Turnitin (global, expensive, limited local pricing)  <br>• Copyleaks AI‑detector (subscription, not locally marketed)  <br>• Free‑online tools (e.g., **GPTZero**, **Writer.com**) – but they are ad‑supported, no bulk API, and no Nigerian pricing. |
| **MONETIZATION FIT** | Universities in Nigeria have modest IT budgets but are *already* paying for plagiarism tools. A low‑cost, locally‑hosted alternative is attractive, though public universities may require a tender process (slow sales cycle). |
| **FIRST WEEK** | 1️⃣ Fork an open‑source AI‑detector repo (e.g., **GPTZero‑API**) and deploy it on the free‑tier EC2 (Docker).  <br>2️⃣ Build a simple web form that accepts a .doc/.pdf upload, runs the detector, and returns a JSON score.  <br>3️⃣ Reach out to two lecturers you know (or via the university’s teaching‑staff WhatsApp) and ask them to test with a sample assignment. |
| **WHY NOW** | The HN post signals a *new* pain point that is just emerging as LLMs become free. No Nigerian‑specific solution exists yet, creating a narrow window before global players localise. |
| **FEASIBILITY SCORES** | **Investment:** 8/10 (free‑tier compute, open‑source model)  <br>**Passive Income:** 3/10 (needs ongoing support, model updates)  <br>**Team Size:** 7/10 (core dev + marketer)  <br>**Time‑to‑Market:** 6/10 (MVP in 3 weeks, model fine‑tuning later)  <br>**Competition:** 4/10 (Turnitin strong, but price barrier)  <br>**Monetization Fit:** 6/10 (education budgets exist, but price‑sensitivity high)  <br>**Regulatory Risk:** 9/10 (no personal data stored if used correctly) |
| **OVERALL SCORE** | **5.9** |

## 3. ClipVault – Curated Public‑Domain Film‑Clip Library for Nigerian Creators

| **Item** | **Details** |
|---|---|
| **PROBLEM** | “A searchable library of forgotten public‑domain film clips from 1915 onward” (HN) – content creators need royalty‑free footage but struggle to find a searchable, mobile‑friendly source. |
| **SOLUTION** | An Android + web app that indexes the Internet Archive’s public‑domain video collection, adds AI‑generated metadata (scene description, keywords, duration), and lets Nigerian creators download 1080p clips directly. The app also offers a “quick‑insert” Chrome extension for YouTube editors. |
| **WHO PAYS** | Small video producers, TikTok/YouTube creators, and ad‑agencies that need royalty‑free B‑roll. |
| **PRICE** | Free tier: 10 clips / day (ad‑supported). <br>Premium: ₦1 500 / month for unlimited downloads, higher‑resolution (4K) and no ads. |
| **EXISTING COMPETITORS** | • **Pexels Video**, **Pixabay**, **Videvo** (free but limited curation, no Nigerian‑specific UI)  <br>• **Storyblocks** (paid, US‑centric)  <br>• Direct search on **archive.org** (no mobile app, poor metadata). |
| **MONETIZATION FIT** | Creators in Nigeria are accustomed to free sources; a modest subscription can work if the UI is truly frictionless. However, ad‑revenue is more realistic than high conversion to paid. |
| **FIRST WEEK** | 1️⃣ Use the Internet Archive’s public API to pull a list of video IDs from the “public‑domain” collection.  <br>2️⃣ Build a simple React web front‑end that shows thumbnails + AI‑generated tags (use AWS Rekognition free tier).  <br>3️⃣ Publish the web version, share the link in local creator Facebook groups, collect feedback on search relevance. |
| **WHY NOW** | Nigerian short‑form video creation is exploding (TikTok, Reels). Creators are actively looking for “free footage” – the Google‑Trends “snack” and “finance” signals show high internet activity, but no local clip‑library exists. |
| **FEASIBILITY SCORES** | **Investment:** 9/10 (free APIs, AWS free tier)  <br>**Passive Income:** 5/10 (ads & subscription, but content updates needed)  <br>**Team Size:** 8/10 (solo‑friendly)  <br>**Time‑to‑Market:** 7/10 (MVP in 10 days)  <br>**Competition:** 5/10 (global free sites, but UI gap)  <br>**Monetization Fit:** 4/10 (price‑sensitive market)  <br>**Regulatory Risk:** 10/10 (public‑domain content) |
| **OVERALL SCORE** | **6.0** |

## 4. SnackScout – Hyper‑local snack discovery & affiliate ordering

| **Item** | **Details** |
|---|---|
| **PROBLEM** | Google‑Trends shows “snack” with > 2 000 search volume in Nigeria, indicating strong curiosity about snack options, yet there is no single app that aggregates local snack brands, prices, and delivery links. |
| **SOLUTION** | A mobile‑first catalogue app that lists popular Nigerian snack brands (e.g., **Chin Chin**, **Plantain chips**, **Bournvita biscuits**), shows user ratings, price comparisons across Jumia, Konga, and local grocery e‑stores, and provides a “one‑tap order” button that redirects to the affiliate link. |
| **WHO PAYS** | Young adults (15‑30 y) who buy snacks online or via delivery; also snack manufacturers that want featured placement. |
| **PRICE** | Free to end‑users.  Revenue: 5 % affiliate commission on each order + optional “Featured Brand” slot ₦5 000 / month. |
| **EXISTING COMPETITORS** | • General e‑commerce apps (Jumia, Konga) – no snack‑only focus.  <br>• Instagram snack‑review pages (manual, no ordering).  <br>• “Snackify” (non‑Nigerian, limited catalogue). |
| **MONETIZATION FIT** | Affiliate commissions are proven in the Nigerian e‑commerce space, but conversion depends heavily on traffic. Brands may pay for placement, but budgets are modest. |
| **FIRST WEEK** | 1️⃣ Scrape the snack category from Jumia’s public API (or use their affiliate program).  <br>2️⃣ Build a simple Android list view with image, price, rating, and a “Buy” button that opens the affiliate URL.  <br>3️⃣ Register for Jumia/Konga affiliate programs, and launch a soft‑launch on the university’s student Discord. |
| **WHY NOW** | The “snack” search spike suggests a fresh wave of interest (possibly tied to exam season). No dedicated snack‑shopping app exists, so early‑mover advantage is real. |
| **FEASIBILITY SCORES** | **Investment:** 9/10 (affiliate APIs are free)  <br>**Passive Income:** 4/10 (needs traffic, but commissions are automated)  <br>**Team Size:** 9/10 (solo‑friendly)  <br>**Time‑to‑Market:** 8/10 (MVP in < 1 week)  <br>**Competition:** 6/10 (indirect competition from big marketplaces)  <br>**Monetization Fit:** 5/10 (affiliate revenue modest, brand fees uncertain)  <br>**Regulatory Risk:** 10/10 (no licensing, just affiliate links) |
| **OVERALL SCORE** | **6.1** |

## 5. ScoreSnap – Real‑time African cricket & football score alerts (mobile‑first)

| **Item** | **Details** |
|---|---|
| **PROBLEM** | Google‑Trends shows frequent queries like “west indies vs india”, “australia vs south africa”, “college football scores from yesterday”. Nigerian sports fans lack a lightweight, push‑notification‑only app that aggregates *all* live scores without heavy data usage. |
| **SOLUTION** | An Android app that pulls live JSON feeds from free APIs (e.g., **cricapi**, **football‑data.org**) and sends customizable push notifications (team‑specific, tournament‑specific). The UI is deliberately minimal – just a list of “Your Teams → Live/Finished”. |
| **WHO PAYS** | Sports‑enthusiasts (15‑45 y) who want instant alerts on cheap phones; advertisers (sports betting, merch) targeting that demographic. |
| **PRICE** | Free to users.  Revenue: native ad placements (≈ ₦150 / 1 000 impressions) + optional “no‑ads” upgrade ₦800 / year. |
| **EXISTING COMPETITORS** | • **ESPN**, **Cricbuzz**, **FlashScore** (global, data‑heavy, many ads).  <br>• Local Facebook groups that post scores manually. |
| **MONETIZATION FIT** | Nigerian users are accustomed to free sports apps; ad‑based model works if you can achieve > 10 k MAU. The premium “no‑ads” tier is low‑conversion (≈ 2 %). |
| **FIRST WEEK** | 1️⃣ Register for free cricket & football JSON APIs, test a simple Node.js endpoint on AWS Lambda (free tier).  <br>2️⃣ Build a bare‑bones Android UI that shows a list of matches and triggers a Firebase Cloud Messaging push when a score changes.  <br>3️⃣ Publish to Play Store, promote via the university’s sports WhatsApp group and ask for feedback. |
| **WHY NOW** | The Google‑Trends list shows multiple *current* match‑up queries, indicating a live interest. Existing global apps are data‑heavy and not optimized for low‑bandwidth Nigerian networks, leaving a niche. |
| **FEASIBILITY SCORES** | **Investment:** 9/10 (free APIs, free Firebase)  <br>**Passive Income:** 5/10 (ads are automated, but need scale)  <br>**Team Size:** 9/10
