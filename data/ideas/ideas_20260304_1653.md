After analyzing the problems from real users, I've identified the top 5 startup opportunities for Nigerian university students. Here are the ideas:

**1. Doomscrolling Detector**
1. **IDEA NAME**: Doomscrolling Detector
2. **PROBLEM**: Many people waste hours scrolling through social media, leading to decreased productivity and increased stress.
3. **SOLUTION**: A simple Android app that tracks and alerts users to their excessive social media scrolling habits, providing tips to reduce screen time.
4. **WHO PAYS**: Students and young professionals aged 18-30 who want to improve their productivity and mental health.
5. **PRICE**: ₦500/month (in-app subscription)
6. **FIRST WEEK**:
	* Step 1: Conduct market research to understand the target audience's social media habits and preferences.
	* Step 2: Design a user-friendly interface for the app using existing design tools.
	* Step 3: Develop a basic version of the app using the Android developer's skills.
7. **WHY NOW**: The "Show HN: I built an app to stop me doomscrolling by touching grass" post on HackerNews highlights the need for a solution to this problem.
8. **FEASIBILITY SCORES**:
	* Investment: 8/10 (uses existing resources, minimal costs)
	* Passive Income: 6/10 (some manual work required for updates and customer support)
	* Team Size: 7/10 (can be developed by a single developer, but marketing help is needed)
	* Time to Market: 8/10 (can be launched within weeks)

**2. Nigerian Product Review Platform**
1. **IDEA NAME**: NaijaReviews
2. **PROBLEM**: Consumers in Nigeria lack a reliable platform to find reviews and ratings for local products, making informed purchasing decisions difficult.
3. **SOLUTION**: A web-based platform that allows users to review and rate Nigerian products, similar to Rotten Tomatoes.
4. **WHO PAYS**: Consumers and businesses looking for authentic product reviews (freemium model, with paid premium features).
5. **PRICE**: ₦1,000/month (premium features, e.g., advanced filtering and sorting)
6. **FIRST WEEK**:
	* Step 1: Research existing review platforms and identify features to include in NaijaReviews.
	* Step 2: Design a basic website layout using web development skills.
	* Step 3: Develop a content management system to handle user reviews and ratings.
7. **WHY NOW**: The "Show HN: I built a Rotten Tomatoes-style platform for durable products" post on HackerNews shows the potential for a similar platform in Nigeria.
8. **FEASIBILITY SCORES**:
	* Investment: 9/10 (uses existing resources, minimal costs)
	* Passive Income: 8/10 (can generate revenue through advertising and premium features)
	* Team Size: 6/10 (requires a team of developers and marketers)
	* Time to Market: 7/10 (can be launched within months)

**3. Open-Source Alternative to Notion**
1. **IDEA NAME**: NaijaNotes
2. **PROBLEM**: Many Nigerians cannot afford or prefer not to use proprietary note-taking and project management tools like Notion.
3. **SOLUTION**: An open-source, web-based alternative to Notion, offering similar features and functionality.
4. **WHO PAYS**: Individuals and businesses looking for a free, customizable note-taking and project management solution (donations and sponsored features).
5. **PRICE**: Free (donations accepted)
6. **FIRST WEEK**:
	* Step 1: Research existing open-source alternatives to Notion and identify features to include in NaijaNotes.
	* Step 2: Design a basic website layout using web development skills.
	* Step 3: Develop a basic version of the platform using open-source technologies.
7. **WHY NOW**: The "Docs – Open source alternative to Notion or Outline" post on HackerNews highlights the demand for open-source alternatives.
8. **FEASIBILITY SCORES**:
	* Investment: 10/10 (uses existing open-source resources, no costs)
	* Passive Income: 5/10 (donations and sponsored features may not generate significant revenue)
	* Team Size: 5/10 (requires a team of developers and contributors)
	* Time to Market: 6/10 (can be launched within months, but development may take longer)

**4. LibreTaxi Nigeria**
1. **IDEA NAME**: LibreTaxi Nigeria
2. **PROBLEM**: Many Nigerians lack access to affordable and reliable transportation options, while existing taxi services can be expensive.
3. **SOLUTION**: A free and open-source alternative to Uber and Lyft, connecting drivers and passengers in Nigeria.
4. **WHO PAYS**: Passengers (per-ride fees) and drivers (commission-based)
5. **PRICE**: ₦500/ride (average fare)
6. **FIRST WEEK**:
	* Step 1: Research the Nigerian transportation market and identify potential partners (e.g., driver associations).
	* Step 2: Design a basic website and mobile app layout using web and Android development skills.
	* Step 3: Develop a basic version of the platform using open-source technologies.
7. **WHY NOW**: The "LibreTaxi – A free and open source alternative to Uber and Lyft" post on HackerNews shows the potential for a similar platform in Nigeria.
8. **FEASIBILITY SCORES**:
	* Investment: 8/10 (uses existing open-source resources, some costs for marketing and partnerships)
	* Passive Income: 7/10 (can generate revenue through commission-based fees)
	* Team Size: 6/10 (requires a team of developers, marketers, and partnerships)
	* Time to Market: 6/10 (can be launched within months, but development and partnerships may take longer)
