## 1. **Fuel‑Watch NG** – Real‑time fuel‑price & station‑finder app  

| **Field** | **Details** |
|---|---|
| **PROBLEM** | Nigerians regularly overpay for petrol because prices change hourly and there is no trustworthy, low‑latency source of station‑by‑station prices. (Google‑Trends shows “gas prices” > 1 000 searches/month; ride‑hailing complaints also mention fare‑inflation due to fuel cost.) |
| **SOLUTION** | Android app + thin web dashboard that crowdsources price uploads from drivers & commuters, validates them with OCR of price‑board photos, and shows the cheapest station within a 5 km radius. Uses AWS Lambda (free tier) for API, DynamoDB for cheap storage, and push notifications for price‑spikes. |
| **WHO PAYS** | Daily commuters & ride‑hailing drivers (≈ 30 % of Nigeria’s working‑age population) who can save ₦200‑₦500 per fill‑up. |
| **PRICE** | Freemium: free basic view; ₦500 / month for “Premium” (price‑alerts, ad‑free, offline cache). |
| **EXISTING COMPETITORS** | *PetrolpriceNG* (website, no mobile app), *Google Maps* (price layer – unreliable), *Local radio bulletins* (manual). No polished Android‑only solution with crowdsourced verification. |
| **MONETIZATION FIT** | Commuters are price‑sensitive but have shown willingness to pay for reliable savings (e.g., paid traffic‑info SMS services in other African markets). Premium price is modest and likely to convert 2‑5 % of active users. |
| **FIRST WEEK** | 1️⃣ Gather a list of 30 high‑traffic petrol stations in Lagos & Abuja; create a simple Google‑Form for price submission. 2️⃣ Build a minimal Android UI (list + map) and a Lambda endpoint that stores submissions in DynamoDB. 3️⃣ Launch a micro‑influencer campaign on Instagram/Twitter (student drivers) to seed the first 200 price reports. |
| **WHY NOW** | Fuel price volatility has spiked after recent subsidy cuts; Google‑Trends confirms rising search interest. Existing solutions are fragmented, and the “price‑lag” complaints in ride‑hailing apps indicate a willingness to adopt a better tool. |
| **FEASIBILITY SCORES** | **Investment:** 9/10 (free AWS tier, no hardware)  **Passive Income:** 6/10 (subscription + ads)  **Team Size:** 9/10 (solo‑friendly)  **Time‑to‑Market:** 8/10 (weeks)  **Competition:** 7/10 (few direct mobile rivals)  **Monetization Fit:** 6/10 (moderate willingness to pay)  **Regulatory Risk:** 9/10 (no licensing, just data aggregation) |

**Average score:** **7.6 / 10** – the strongest overall.

## 2. **Ride‑Receipt Guard** – Automatic fare‑audit & dispute helper for Uber/Bolt users  

| **Field** | **Details** |
|---|---|
| **PROBLEM** | Nigerian riders complain that the Uber/Bolt apps show inconsistent fares, hidden surcharges, and driver‑scams (“fare is false”, “price changes after booking”). |
| **SOLUTION** | Android app that records the GPS trace of every ride, pulls the official fare estimate via the public Uber/Bolt APIs (or scrapes the receipt email), and flags any deviation > 10 %. Generates a pre‑filled dispute email and stores a PDF receipt for the user. |
| **WHO PAYS** | Frequent ride‑hailing users (students, young professionals) who take ≥ 2 rides/week and have spent > ₦5 000/month on rides. |
| **PRICE** | One‑time purchase: ₦1 200 (≈ $2.5) or subscription: ₦300 / month for unlimited audits & priority support. |
| **EXISTING COMPETITORS** | None in Nigeria. Internationally, “FareCheck” (US) exists but is not available on Android and does not support Uber’s Nigerian API. |
| **MONETIZATION FIT** | Ride‑hailing users are already paying per‑ride; a small “insurance‑style” fee for audit protection is a known model (e.g., “Ride‑Shield” in Kenya). Willingness to pay is modest but credible for power users. |
| **FIRST WEEK** | 1️⃣ Reverse‑engineer the Uber receipt email format (publicly available) and build a parser in the API server. 2️⃣ Create a background location‑tracking service that logs start/end GPS points. 3️⃣ Publish a landing page on a free sub‑domain, collect email sign‑ups, and run a small Facebook‑ad test targeting Lagos ride‑hailing groups. |
| **WHY NOW** | The Play‑Store complaints list > 10 distinct grievances about fare transparency. Uber’s recent price‑surge after fuel‑price hikes has amplified the pain point. |
| **FEASIBILITY SCORES** | **Investment:** 8/10 (free tier, no external data cost)  **Passive Income:** 5/10 (requires ongoing support)  **Team Size:** 8/10 (solo‑friendly)  **Time‑to‑Market:** 7/10 (≈ 3 weeks)  **Competition:** 8/10 (practically none)  **Monetization Fit:** 5/10 (price‑sensitive segment)  **Regulatory Risk:** 9/10 (no money‑transfer, just data) |

**Average score:** **6.7 / 10**.

## 3. **Campus‑Swap** – Peer‑to‑peer textbook & notes marketplace for Nigerian universities  

| **Field** | **Details** |
|---|---|
| **PROBLEM** | Students repeatedly complain about the high cost of textbooks and the difficulty of finding up‑to‑date lecture notes (“I built a Git‑tracked book production pipeline”). |
| **SOLUTION** | Web‑app + Android client where verified students list used textbooks, PDFs of lecture notes, and past exam papers. Sellers set price; the platform holds the transaction in an escrow‑style “credit” system (points) to avoid cash handling. Integration with the university’s single‑sign‑on (SSO) for verification. |
| **WHO PAYS** | Undergraduate & postgraduate students (≈ 2 M in Nigeria) who need affordable learning material. |
| **PRICE** | Transaction fee: 5 % of sale price (capped at ₦200). Optional “Premium Seller” badge: ₦1 000 / month for higher visibility. |
| **EXISTING COMPETITORS** | *Jumia Books* (retail, high price), *Konga* (new books only), informal Facebook groups (no escrow, high fraud risk). No dedicated, university‑verified marketplace. |
| **MONETIZATION FIT** | Students are price‑sensitive but have shown willingness to pay small fees for safety (e.g., paid tutoring platforms). A 5 % fee on a typical ₦2 000 textbook yields ₦100 per transaction – viable at scale. |
| **FIRST WEEK** | 1️⃣ Contact the student union of one university (e.g., University of Lagos) to get a list of verified student emails. 2️⃣ Spin up a simple MERN‑style web app on AWS Amplify (free tier) with login via university SSO. 3️⃣ Seed the marketplace with 10‑15 textbook listings from the founders and promote via the university’s WhatsApp groups. |
| **WHY NOW** | The pandemic forced many to rely on digital notes; the “Git‑tracked book” hack shows a DIY mindset that can be channeled into a marketplace. Existing solutions are fragmented and lack trust. |
| **FEASIBILITY SCORES** | **Investment:** 7/10 (free hosting, but need SSO integration)  **Passive Income:** 6/10 (transaction fees are automated)  **Team Size:** 7/10 (needs modest moderation)  **Time‑to‑Market:** 6/10 (≈ 4‑5 weeks for SSO)  **Competition:** 6/10 (some informal groups, but no direct platform)  **Monetization Fit:** 6/10 (students pay small fees)  **Regulatory Risk:** 8/10 (no regulated goods, just used books) |

**Average score:** **6.3 / 10**.

## 4. **Quick‑Fix NG** – Verified local‑service marketplace (plumbers, electricians, AC techs)  

| **Field** | **Details** |
|---|---|
| **PROBLEM** | Nigerians repeatedly encounter “dodgy drivers” and “scam” service providers (see Play‑Store complaints about Uber & ride‑share scams). The same trust issue exists for home‑service providers. |
| **SOLUTION** | Android + web platform where service professionals are vetted (ID + background check via a simple questionnaire) and earn a “Quick‑Fix” badge. Users request a service, receive 3 vetted quotes, and can pay via mobile money (e.g., Paga, OPay). The app stores a rating history to discourage fraud. |
| **WHO PAYS** | Urban middle‑class households (≈ 1 M) who need occasional repairs and are willing to pay for guaranteed quality. |
| **PRICE** | 10 % commission on each job (minimum ₦500). Optional “Premium Provider” subscription: ₦2 000 / month for top‑of‑search placement. |
| **EXISTING COMPETITORS** | *Jumia Services* (limited to major cities), *OyaPay* (handyman on‑demand but no vetting), informal WhatsApp groups. No dedicated vetting‑first marketplace. |
| **MONETIZATION FIT** | Home‑service users in Nigeria already spend ₦5 000‑₦20 000 per job and are accustomed to paying a commission for convenience (e.g., Uber). The commission model is proven. |
| **FIRST WEEK** | 1️⃣ Identify 20 local tradespeople willing to pilot the platform (offer them free first‑job). 2️⃣ Create a simple booking flow in Android (Google Maps API for location, Stripe‑compatible payment gateway for mobile money). 3️⃣ Launch a pilot in one Lagos neighbourhood and collect NPS feedback. |
| **WHY NOW** | Trust fatigue is evident across ride‑hailing and other on‑demand services; a vetted marketplace leverages that pain point. The mobile‑money ecosystem in Nigeria is mature, making payments easy. |
| **FEASIBILITY SCORES** | **Investment:** 7/10 (free tier, but need payment gateway integration)  **Passive Income:** 5/10 (needs ongoing matching)  **Team Size:** 6/10 (needs customer support)  **Time‑to‑Market:** 5/10 (≈ 6 weeks for vetting workflow)  **Competition:** 5/10 (several players, but none with strong vetting)  **Monetization Fit:** 7/10 (commission model works)  **Regulatory Risk:** 6/10 (some liability if a technician damages property) |

**Average score:** **5.9 / 10**.

## 5. **Lite‑Ride** – Ultra‑light ride‑hailing client for low‑end Android phones  

| **Field** | **Details** |
|---|---|
| **PROBLEM** | Many Nigerian riders complain that the Uber/Bolt apps are “laggy” and crash on low‑spec phones (“app lagg and only disappointment”). |
| **SOLUTION** | A stripped‑down Android client that only implements core functions: location, request, driver‑track, and payment via deep‑link to the official app. Uses minimal UI libraries (< 2 MB) and works on Android 5+. Acts as a “front‑end” that forwards the request to Uber/Bolt via their public deep‑link URLs. |
| **WHO PAYS** | Low‑income commuters (≈ 4 M) who own Android 5‑7 devices and cannot run the full Uber app. |
| **PRICE** | Free with non‑intrusive banner ads (≈ ₦50 / impression). Optional ad‑free version: ₦300 / month. |
| **EXISTING COMPETITORS** | None in Nigeria. International “Uber Lite” exists in some markets but is not available on the Play Store for Nigeria. |
| **MONETIZATION FIT** | This segment is extremely price‑sensitive; ad‑supported free model is realistic, but conversion to paid is expected to be < 2 %. |
| **FIRST WEEK** | 1️⃣ Fork the open‑source “Uber Lite” Android code (GitHub) and replace region‑specific URLs. 2️⃣ Test on a low‑end device (e.g., Tecno Camon 10) for performance. 3️⃣ Publish to Play Store, promote via low‑budget TikTok/WhatsApp videos targeting “budget phone” groups. |
| **WHY NOW** | The Play‑Store complaints list a clear pain point (laggy ride‑hailing apps). Android low‑end penetration in Nigeria is > 60 %, creating a sizable addressable market that the official apps ignore. |
| **FEASIBILITY SCORES** | **Investment:** 9/10 (code reuse, free hosting)  **Passive Income:** 4/10 (ad revenue is low‑margin)  **Team Size:** 9/10 (solo‑friendly)  **Time‑to‑Market:** 9/10 (days)  **Competition:** 8/10 (no direct competitor locally)  **Monetization Fit:** 4/10 (price‑sensitive, ad‑only)  **Regulatory Risk:** 9/10 (no data handling beyond what Uber already does) |

**Average score:** **5.7 / 10**.
