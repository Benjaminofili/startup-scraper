## 1. CAMPUS‑RIDE (University‑only ride‑share)

| **Item** | **Details** |
|---|---|
| **PROBLEM** | “Uber … takes unnecessary wait time, cancels at the last minute, and the app crashes” – repeated complaints from Play Store reviews (e.g., “driver cancelled at the last …”, “worst app I have ever used”). Students need a reliable, cheap way to get from dorms to lectures or parties on campus. |
| **SOLUTION** | A lightweight Android app that lets **students** post a ride request and **student drivers** (who already own a bike, motorbike or car) accept it. Payments are cash‑only or via mobile‑money (e.g., OPay, Paga). The platform only operates inside a single university’s perimeter, so no licensing, no insurance, and no need to integrate with Uber/Bolt APIs. |
| **WHO PAYS** | • **Riders** – students who value punctuality over price (they’ll pay a modest flat fee per ride, e.g., ₦300‑₦500). <br>• **Driver‑partners** – pay a small subscription (₦1 000 / month) for the “lead‑generation” service. |
| **PRICE** | • Rider: ₦350 per 5 km ride (pay‑as‑you‑go). <br>• Driver subscription: ₦1 000 / month (unlimited ride leads). |
| **EXISTING COMPETITORS** | • Uber / Bolt (national, high‑price, unreliable on campuses). <br>• Informal “hail‑a‑rickshaw” on the streets (no app, cash‑only, no safety rating). |
| **MONETIZATION FIT** | University students in Nigeria **do spend on transport** (average daily spend ≈ ₦1 200). They are price‑sensitive but will pay a small premium for guaranteed pickup. Driver‑partner subscription is realistic because drivers already pay for “lead” services (e.g., taxi‑dispatch SMS groups). |
| **FIRST WEEK** | 1. **Validate demand** – run a 2‑day paper‑survey in one campus (e.g., University of Lagos) and collect 50+ “I’d use an app like this” sign‑ups. <br>2. **Build MVP** – a simple Android list‑view + Firebase‑free‑tier backend for ride posting & acceptance (≈ 2 days). <br>3. **Launch a closed beta** – invite the surveyed students, pay the first 5 rides out‑of‑pocket to seed trust, and start collecting feedback. |
| **WHY NOW** | The complaints show a **clear pain point** with existing mega‑apps on campuses, and the Nigerian “cash‑first” culture still favours peer‑to‑peer solutions that don’t need heavy regulation. |
| **FEASIBILITY SCORES** | **Investment:** 9/10 (no cash needed beyond free tier) <br>**Passive Income:** 5/10 (needs daily driver‑support & dispute handling) <br>**Team Size:** 8/10 (3 devs can handle Android + simple API) <br>**Time to Market:** 8/10 (MVP in < 1 week) <br>**Competition:** 4/10 (Uber/Bolt are strong, but campus‑only focus is a niche) <br>**Monetization Fit:** 6/10 (students pay small fees, drivers pay subscription) <br>**Regulatory Risk:** 8/10 (no vehicle‑ownership licensing required; just a matching service) |

## 2. DRIVER‑HUB (Multi‑platform order manager for ride‑share drivers)

| **Item** | **Details** |
|---|---|
| **PROBLEM** | Drivers on Uber/Bolt complain about “cancellations”, “no payment pop‑up”, “crashes” – they waste time switching between apps, lose orders, and can’t see their earnings in one place. |
| **SOLUTION** | A **web‑+‑Android dashboard** that aggregates a driver’s Uber, Bolt, and any local “auto‑rickshaw” booking feeds (via the publicly‑available driver‑partner APIs or simple screen‑scraping). The driver sees a single list of incoming rides, can accept/reject, and the app logs earnings automatically. |
| **WHO PAYS** | **Ride‑share drivers** (full‑time or part‑time) who want to increase accepted rides and reduce “ghost” cancellations. |
| **PRICE** | Subscription: ₦2 000 / month (≈ $4) for unlimited aggregation. A 14‑day free trial to prove ROI. |
| **EXISTING COMPETITORS** | • “Driver‑Dashboard” extensions on Chrome (free, manual). <br>• Local SMS‑based dispatch groups (informal, no UI). <br>• No polished, multi‑platform mobile app in Nigeria. |
| **MONETIZATION FIT** | Drivers already pay for **fuel**, **vehicle maintenance**, and sometimes **SMS‑dispatch fees**. A ₦2 000 monthly tool that can increase completed rides by even 5 % is a clear net‑gain. However, many drivers are cash‑only and may be reluctant to subscribe without a proven ROI. |
| **FIRST WEEK** | 1. **Interview 10 drivers** (Uber & Bolt) to map their exact workflow and confirm willingness to pay. <br>2. **Build a prototype** that pulls Uber driver‑partner data (using Uber’s sandbox) and displays it on a simple React‑Native screen. <br>3. **Deploy to a private Play Store track**, give access to the interviewed drivers, and measure ride‑acceptance lift over 3 days. |
| **WHY NOW** | The flood of negative reviews (crashes, payment glitches) shows drivers are **frustrated now** and looking for any tool that can reduce friction. Uber’s API for drivers is still “beta” and not widely used in Nigeria, leaving a gap. |
| **FEASIBILITY SCORES** | **Investment:** 8/10 (free‑tier API calls, no hardware) <br>**Passive Income:** 4/10 (needs ongoing support, driver onboarding) <br>**Team Size:** 7/10 (needs API integration work) <br>**Time to Market:** 6/10 (API approvals may take a week) <br>**Competition:** 7/10 (very few direct competitors) <br>**Monetization Fit:** 5/10 (price‑sensitive drivers) <br>**Regulatory Risk:** 9/10 (pure software, no licensing) |

## 3. NAJIA‑VOICE (Offline speech‑to‑text for Nigerian languages)

| **Item** | **Details** |
|---|---|
| **PROBLEM** | HackerNews post “Ghost Pepper – Local hold‑to‑talk speech‑to‑text for macOS” shows a demand for **offline, low‑latency speech‑to‑text**. In Nigeria, many users have spotty data and need **local language** (Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa) transcription for journalism, note‑taking, and content creation. |
| **SOLUTION** | An Android app that ships a **tiny open‑source acoustic model** (e.g., Whisper‑tiny) **quantised to run on‑device** and fine‑tuned on publicly‑available Yoruba/Igbo/Hausa corpora. The UI is a single “hold‑to‑talk” button that writes the transcript into any other app via Android’s share‑intent. |
| **WHO PAYS** | • **Student journalists** and **content creators** who need quick captions without data costs. <br>• **SMEs** that produce short audio notes (e.g., field agents). |
| **PRICE** | One‑time purchase: ₦1 500 (≈ $3). Optional premium add‑on: ₦500 for “custom dictionary” (e.g., local slang). |
| **EXISTING COMPETITORS** | • Google Speech‑to‑Text (cloud, data‑heavy, English‑only). <br>• Whisper‑based Android apps (mostly English). <br>• No dedicated offline Nigerian‑language STT app. |
| **MONETIZATION FIT** | Users in this niche **are willing to pay** for offline capability because data is expensive (≈ ₦1 500 / GB). However the market size is modest; the price point must stay low. |
| **FIRST WEEK** | 1. **Collect 2 k short audio clips** in Yoruba/Igbo/Hausa from open‑source datasets (e.g., Common Voice). <br>2. **Fine‑tune Whisper‑tiny** on a free GPU (Google Colab) and export a quantised model. <br>3. **Wrap the model in a minimal Android app** (single activity) and publish to the Play Store internal test track. |
| **WHY NOW** | Data costs are rising, and the **HackerNews trend** shows developers are actively building local offline STT solutions. Nigeria lacks any such product for local languages, creating a first‑mover advantage. |
| **FEASIBILITY SCORES** | **Investment:** 9/10 (free compute, open‑source models) <br>**Passive Income:** 6/10 (one‑time sales, low support) <br>**Team Size:** 8/10 (single Android dev + ML‑dev) <br>**Time to Market:** 5/10 (model fine‑tuning + QA may take 2‑3 weeks) <br>**Competition:** 7/10 (no direct local language competitor) <br>**Monetization Fit:** 5/10 (price‑sensitive, small niche) <br>**Regulatory Risk:** 10/10 (no personal data stored, fully offline) |

## 4. UNI‑NOTES (AI‑assisted personal knowledge base for Nigerian students)

| **Item** | **Details** |
|---|---|
| **PROBLEM** | HackerNews “OpenKnowledge – open source AI‑first alternative to Obsidian/Notion” demonstrates demand for **AI‑enhanced note‑taking**. Nigerian university students struggle with fragmented lecture PDFs, PDFs in low‑quality scans, and no cheap “smart notebook”. |
| **SOLUTION** | A **web‑app + Android companion** that lets students upload PDFs, images, or voice recordings. An **open‑source LLM** (e.g., Llama‑2‑7B) runs on AWS free‑tier (via Lambda + S3) to generate **summaries, flashcards, and searchable embeddings**. The UI mimics Notion but is stripped down to the essentials (no paid plugins). |
| **WHO PAYS** | **Undergraduate & postgraduate students** (especially in engineering, medicine, law) who need to study efficiently for exams. |
| **PRICE** | Freemium: <br>• Free tier – up to 5 documents, 100 MB storage. <br>• Premium – ₦2 500 / month for unlimited docs, 5 GB storage, and priority AI processing. |
| **EXISTING COMPETITORS** | • Notion (free tier, but AI features are paid & not tailored to Nigerian curricula). <br>• Obsidian (free desktop, no AI). <br>• Local “Google Drive + Docs” combos (manual summarisation). |
| **MONETIZATION FIT** | Nigerian students **do spend on study aids** (e.g., past‑question banks). A ₦2 500 monthly price is comparable to a private tutor session; however many students rely on free tools, so conversion will be modest (target 5‑10 % of active free users). |
| **FIRST WEEK** | 1. **Survey 200 students** across two campuses to confirm willingness to pay for AI‑summaries. <br>2. **Deploy a minimal Flask API** on AWS Lambda (free tier) that runs a tiny LLM (e.g., Mistral‑7B‑instruct quantised) for summarisation. <br>3. **Build a simple React front‑end** (hosted on GitHub Pages) and an Android “upload‑file” wrapper; open a closed‑beta with the surveyed students. |
| **WHY NOW** | The **global hype for AI‑note‑taking** is at its peak, and the free‑tier AWS credits make the compute cost negligible for a small user base. No local competitor offers AI‑summaries in a student‑friendly package. |
| **FEASIBILITY SCORES** | **Investment:** 8/10 (AWS free tier covers compute for a few hundred users) <br>**Passive Income:** 4/10 (needs ongoing model updates & support) <br>**Team Size:** 7/10 (needs both web & Android dev) <br>**Time to Market:** 5/10 (model integration takes ~2 weeks) <br>**Competition:** 5/10 (global players exist, but not localized) <br>**Monetization Fit:** 4/10 (students are price‑sensitive) <br>**Regulatory Risk:** 9/10 (no personal data stored beyond user‑uploaded docs) |
