## 1. **Ride‑Help NG** – a “complaint‑to‑refund” hub for ride‑hailing users  

| **Item** | **Details** |
|---|---|
| **PROBLEM** | Users repeatedly complain on Play Store reviews that Uber/Bolt drivers cancel, over‑charge, or provide terrible customer service (e.g., “Pathetic experience at airport. Driver canceled after making me wait for 15 mins”). |
| **SOLUTION** | A lightweight Android/web app where riders can (a) upload a ride receipt, (b) tag the issue (cancellation, price‑jump, driver misconduct), and (c) automatically generate a pre‑filled support ticket that is sent to the ride‑hailing provider **and** stored on a public “track‑record” ledger. The app also offers a simple escrow‑style “refund‑request” button that triggers a push‑notification to the driver and the platform, nudging faster resolution. |
| **WHO PAYS** | • **Power users** (frequent riders, corporate travel managers) who need a reliable way to claim refunds.<br>• **Ride‑hailing platforms** (Uber, Bolt) that want a white‑label “complaint‑automation” widget to reduce support load. |
| **PRICE** | • **B2C** – ₦2 000 / month for a “Premium Refund‑Accelerator” (auto‑escalation, SMS alerts).<br>• **B2B** – ₦15 000 / month per city for a branded widget embedded in the platform’s driver‑app. |
| **EXISTING COMPETITORS** | • Uber’s own in‑app support (very low success rate).<br>• “RideSafe” (Nigeria‑focused but only a WhatsApp bot, no escrow).<br>• Generic complaint‑aggregators (e.g., Trustpilot) – not integrated with ride‑hailing APIs. |
| **MONETIZATION FIT** | Power users in Nigeria are **price‑sensitive** but willing to pay for guaranteed refunds (≈ 5 % of a typical ₦1 500 ride). Platforms have a **budget for CS automation** and have already paid for similar SaaS tools in other markets. |
| **FIRST WEEK** | 1️⃣ Scrape the most recent Play Store complaints (the 7 items you listed) to build a “pain‑point taxonomy”.<br>2️⃣ Build a minimal Android UI (receipt upload + issue picker) and a thin Node.js API on AWS Free Tier that stores tickets in DynamoDB.<br>3️⃣ Publish the app on the Play Store, push it to university WhatsApp groups and to local ride‑hailing forums for beta testers. |
| **WHY NOW** | The complaints are **fresh (2024‑2025)** and the ride‑hailing market in Nigeria is still expanding; platforms are scrambling to improve CS metrics after a wave of negative reviews. |
| **FEASIBILITY SCORES** | **Investment:** 9/10 (all cloud services free tier, no hardware).<br>**Passive Income:** 5/10 (requires ongoing ticket triage for B2C, but B2B widget can be largely automated).<br>**Team Size:** 8/10 (3 devs can cover Android, API, and a simple admin dashboard).<br>**Time‑to‑Market:** 8/10 (MVP in <2 weeks).<br>**Competition:** 6/10 (existing in‑app support is weak, but big players could copy).<br>**Monetization Fit:** 6/10 (B2B more reliable than B2C).<br>**Regulatory Risk:** 9/10 (no licensing, just consumer‑rights facilitation). |
| **OVERALL SCORE (average)** | **7.3** |

## 2. **Subsidy‑Scout** – a “government‑subsidy discovery & claim assistant”  

| **Item** | **Details** |
|---|---|
| **PROBLEM** | Google Trends shows a spike in searches for “subsidy” (≈ 1 000 + monthly volume) in Nigeria, indicating people are looking for information on fuel, fertilizer, or cash‑transfer subsidies but struggle to locate the right portal or eligibility criteria. |
| **SOLUTION** | A searchable Android/web app that aggregates **all federal & state subsidy programmes** (fuel, fertilizer, N-Power, etc.). Users input a few personal data points (state, occupation, income bracket) and the app instantly tells them which subsidies they qualify for, provides direct links to the official claim forms, and optionally auto‑fills the forms (via the public APIs that ministries expose). Push notifications remind users of deadlines. |
| **WHO PAYS** | • **Small‑business owners & farmers** who need to claim fertilizer or fuel subsidies.<br>• **NGOs & extension services** that want a branded version for their members (white‑label). |
| **PRICE** | • **B2C** – Free tier (basic lookup).<br>• **Premium** – ₦1 500 / month for auto‑fill + deadline SMS alerts.<br>• **B2B/NGO** – ₦10 000 / month per 5 000 users (white‑label). |
| **EXISTING COMPETITORS** | • Government portals (e.g., **FIRS**, **Ministry of Agriculture**) – fragmented, not mobile‑first.<br>• “GovtAid” (a small Facebook page) – manual posting, no automation.<br>• No dedicated Nigerian subsidy‑aggregator app. |
| **MONETIZATION FIT** | Farmers and SMEs **do pay** for services that guarantee they receive cash‑back or reduced input costs; a ₦1 500 subscription is modest compared with the value of a ₦20 000‑₦100 000 subsidy. NGOs already allocate budgets for member‑support tools. |
| **FIRST WEEK** | 1️⃣ Compile a spreadsheet of the top 5‑7 subsidy programmes (official URLs, eligibility rules).<br>2️⃣ Build a simple React web front‑end + Node.js API that returns matching programmes based on user input.<br>3️⃣ Wrap the web UI in a thin Android WebView and publish to the Play Store for beta testing with local farmer groups. |
| **WHY NOW** | The **“subsidy” search surge** is tied to recent policy announcements (fuel price adjustments, fertilizer subsidies) – a narrow window where users are actively seeking help. |
| **FEASIBILITY SCORES** | **Investment:** 9/10 (free data, free tier hosting).<br>**Passive Income:** 7/10 (once the lookup engine is built, revenue is subscription‑based).<br>**Team Size:** 8/10 (3 devs can handle front‑end, API, and data‑scraping).<br>**Time‑to‑Market:** 7/10 (data collection takes a few days).<br>**Competition:** 8/10 (no direct app competitor).<br>**Monetization Fit:** 7/10 (clear value‑to‑price ratio).<br>**Regulatory Risk:** 9/10 (just aggregating public info). |
| **OVERALL SCORE** | **7.6** |

## 3. **Candidate‑Pulse** – a fact‑checked, searchable candidate‑profile app for Nigerian elections  

| **Item** | **Details** |
|---|---|
| **PROBLEM** | Google Trends shows a solid “candidate” search volume (≈ 1 000 + monthly) in Nigeria, reflecting voter confusion about who is running and what they stand for. Existing information is scattered across party websites, news sites, and social media, often biased. |
| **SOLUTION** | An Android/web app that **curates verified candidate bios, manifestos, and voting records** (where available). Users can filter by state, party, or issue (education, health). Each entry includes a “trust‑score” derived from cross‑checking three independent sources (e.g., INEC, reputable news outlets, fact‑check NGOs). Users can bookmark, share, and receive push alerts when a candidate updates their platform. |
| **WHO PAYS** | • **Urban, first‑time voters** (18‑35) who use smartphones for political research.<br>• **Civic NGOs** that want a branded version for voter‑education campaigns. |
| **PRICE** | • **Free** for basic browsing.<br>• **Premium** – ₦1 200 / month for ad‑free experience, offline download of state‑specific data, and early‑access to “candidate‑Q&A” webinars. |
| **EXISTING COMPETITORS** | • **INFORMATION** – the INEC website (not mobile‑friendly).<br>• **Nairaland political threads** (unstructured).<br>• **“Nigerian Election Tracker”** (a simple news‑aggregator app, no verification). |
| **MONETIZATION FIT** | Young Nigerians **spend on news & political content** (e.g., premium newsletters). A modest ₦1 200 subscription is acceptable if the app becomes a trusted source. NGOs already purchase civic‑engagement tools, making the B2B angle viable. |
| **FIRST WEEK** | 1️⃣ Scrape the INEC candidate list for the upcoming election (public CSV).<br>2️⃣ Build a minimal API that stores candidate data and a simple React Native front‑end that shows a list + detail view.<br>3️⃣ Reach out to a university political‑science club to pilot the app and collect feedback. |
| **WHY NOW** | Election cycles in Nigeria generate **spikes in “candidate” searches**; the next general election is within 12 months, creating a time‑bound demand for reliable info. |
| **FEASIBILITY SCORES** | **Investment:** 9/10 (public data, free hosting).<br>**Passive Income:** 6/10 (requires periodic content updates, but subscription can be automated).<br>**Team Size:** 8/10 (data ingestion + UI).<br>**Time‑to‑Market:** 7/10 (data cleaning takes a few days).<br>**Competition:** 6/10 (some news apps exist but lack verification).<br>**Monetization Fit:** 5/10 (young voters are price‑sensitive; NGOs are the stronger revenue source).<br>**Regulatory Risk:** 8/10 (political content can attract scrutiny, but using only public data mitigates risk). |
| **OVERALL SCORE** | **6.9** |

| **SOLUTION** | A web portal (and companion Android app) that sells **downloadable, one‑click n8n workflow packs** for common Nigerian business scenarios: “auto‑send payment receipt via WhatsApp after Paystack transaction”, “sync new Shopify orders to local inventory spreadsheet”, “trigger SMS alert on low‑stock via Twilio”.
