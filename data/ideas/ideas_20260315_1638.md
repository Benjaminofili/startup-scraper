After analyzing the problems from real users, I've identified the top 5 startup opportunities for Nigerian university students. Here are the ideas:

**1. Doomscrolling Detector**
1. **IDEA NAME**: Doomscrolling Detector
2. **PROBLEM**: Many people, especially students, struggle with excessive social media scrolling, affecting their mental health and productivity.
3. **SOLUTION**: A mobile app that tracks and alerts users about their social media usage, providing tips to reduce doomscrolling.
4. **WHO PAYS**: Students and young professionals aged 18-30, who are active on social media.
5. **PRICE**: ₦500/month (in-app subscription)
6. **FIRST WEEK**:
	* Step 1: Conduct market research to understand the target audience's social media habits.
	* Step 2: Design a simple, user-friendly app interface using existing APIs (e.g., Google's Digital Wellbeing API).
	* Step 3: Develop a basic version of the app using the Android developer on the team.
7. **WHY NOW**: The problem of doomscrolling is mentioned in the HackerNews post "Show HN: I built an app to stop me doomscrolling by touching grass", indicating a growing concern about this issue.
8. **FEASIBILITY SCORES**:
	* Investment: 8/10 (uses existing APIs and free design tools)
	* Passive Income: 6/10 (requires some maintenance and updates)
	* Team Size: 8/10 (can be developed with a small team)
	* Time to Market: 8/10 (can be launched within a few weeks)

**2. LibreRide - A Free Ride-Hailing Alternative**
1. **IDEA NAME**: LibreRide
2. **PROBLEM**: Many Nigerians struggle with the high costs of ride-hailing services like Uber and Bolt.
3. **SOLUTION**: A free and open-source ride-hailing platform that connects drivers with passengers, using a peer-to-peer model.
4. **WHO PAYS**: None (the platform is free, with optional donations)
5. **PRICE**: ₦0 (free to use)
6. **FIRST WEEK**:
	* Step 1: Research existing open-source ride-hailing platforms (e.g., LibreTaxi) and adapt them for the Nigerian market.
	* Step 2: Develop a basic web and mobile interface for the platform using the web and Android developers.
	* Step 3: Establish partnerships with local driver associations and unions to promote the platform.
7. **WHY NOW**: The HackerNews post "LibreTaxi – A free and open source alternative to Uber and Lyft" shows that there is interest in free and open-source ride-hailing solutions.
8. **FEASIBILITY SCORES**:
	* Investment: 9/10 (uses existing open-source solutions and free marketing)
	* Passive Income: 5/10 (requires some maintenance and updates)
	* Team Size: 7/10 (requires a small team with diverse skills)
	* Time to Market: 7/10 (can be launched within a few months)
