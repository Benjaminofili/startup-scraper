After analyzing the problems from real users on HackerNews, I've identified the top 5 startup opportunities for Nigerian university students. Here are the ideas:

**1. Open Source Note-Taking App**
1. **IDEA NAME**: NoteHub
2. **PROBLEM**: Many users are looking for open-source alternatives to Notion or Outline for note-taking.
3. **SOLUTION**: A simple, open-source note-taking app with features like tagging, searching, and organization.
4. **WHO PAYS**: Students and professionals who need a reliable note-taking app.
5. **PRICE**: Free, with optional donations (₦500/month).
6. **FIRST WEEK**:
	* Step 1: Research existing open-source note-taking apps and identify key features to include.
	* Step 2: Design a basic user interface and user experience for the app.
	* Step 3: Set up a GitHub repository and start building the app using existing open-source libraries.
7. **WHY NOW**: Many users are looking for alternatives to proprietary note-taking apps, and an open-source solution can fill this gap.
8. **FEASIBILITY SCORES**:
	* Investment: 9/10 (₦0 needed, except for potential server costs)
	* Passive Income: 8/10 (donations and potential ads)
	* Team Size: 8/10 (can be built by a solo developer, but a team can help with maintenance)
	* Time to Market: 6/10 (will take some time to build and test, but can be launched in a few weeks)

**2. Personal Finance Tracker**
1. **IDEA NAME**: FinancePal
2. **PROBLEM**: Many Nigerians struggle to track their expenses and stay on top of their finances.
3. **SOLUTION**: A simple, user-friendly personal finance tracker app that allows users to track expenses, income, and budgets.
4. **WHO PAYS**: Individuals and small business owners who need help managing their finances.
5. **PRICE**: Free, with optional premium features (₦1,000/month).
6. **FIRST WEEK**:
	* Step 1: Research existing personal finance apps and identify key features to include.
	* Step 2: Design a user interface and user experience for the app.
	* Step 3: Start building the app using a framework like Flutter or React Native.
7. **WHY NOW**: With the rise of digital payments in Nigeria, there is a growing need for personal finance management tools.
8. **FEASIBILITY SCORES**:
	* Investment: 8/10 (some initial investment in marketing and server costs)
	* Passive Income: 7/10 (premium features and ads)
	* Team Size: 6/10 (a small team of 2-3 developers and a designer)
	* Time to Market: 5/10 (will take some time to build and test, but can be launched in a few weeks)

**3. E-Learning Platform for Nigerian Students**
1. **IDEA NAME**: LearnNaija
2. **PROBLEM**: Many Nigerian students struggle to access quality educational resources and study materials.
3. **SOLUTION**: An e-learning platform that provides access to study materials, online courses, and educational resources tailored to the Nigerian curriculum.
4. **WHO PAYS**: Students, parents, and schools.
5. **PRICE**: ₦2,000/month (individual subscription), ₦5,000/month (school subscription).
6. **FIRST WEEK**:
	* Step 1: Research existing e-learning platforms and identify key features to include.
	* Step 2: Partner with educators and content creators to develop study materials and courses.
	* Step 3: Start building the platform using a framework like WordPress or Moodle.
7. **WHY NOW**: With the growing demand for online learning in Nigeria, there is a need for a platform that caters to Nigerian students.
8. **FEASIBILITY SCORES**:
	* Investment: 6/10 (some initial investment in content creation and marketing)
	* Passive Income: 8/10 (subscription-based model)
	* Team Size: 7/10 (a team of 3-4 developers, educators, and marketers)
	* Time to Market: 4/10 (will take some time to build and test, but can be launched in a few months)

**5. Digital Marketplace for Nigerian Artisans**
1. **IDEA NAME**: ArtisanHub
2. **PROBLEM**: Many Nigerian artisans struggle to reach a wider audience and sell their products online.
3. **SOLUTION**: A digital marketplace that connects Nigerian artisans with customers and provides a platform for them to sell their products.
4. **WHO PAYS**: Artisans, customers, and advertisers.
5. **PRICE**: Commission-based (10% per sale), with optional premium features (₦2,000/month).
6. **FIRST WEEK**:
	* Step 1: Research existing marketplaces and identify key features to include.
	* Step 2: Partner with artisans and content creators to develop a catalog of products.
	* Step 3: Start building the platform using a framework like Shopify or WooCommerce.
7. **WHY NOW**: With the growing demand for e-commerce in Nigeria, there is a need for a platform that caters to Nigerian artisans.
8. **FEASIBILITY SCORES**:
	* Investment: 5/10 (some initial investment in marketing and server costs)
	* Passive Income: 7/10 (commission-based model and ads)
	* Team Size: 6/10 (a team of 3-4 developers, marketers, and artisans)
	* Time to Market: 4/10 (will take some time to build and test, but can be launched in a few months)

These ideas prioritize zero-capital, passive income opportunities and focus on the Nigerian market. They also address specific pain points and needs in the market, making them more likely to succeed.
