## 1. RideGuard – “Uber / Bolt Fare & Ride‑Audit”

**PROBLEM**  
Passengers claim the app shows them a lower fare than the driver receives, and sometimes the ride never arrives (Play Store complaints).

**SOLUTION**  
An Android app that (a) records GPS + timestamp from the moment a ride is requested, (b) pulls the driver‑estimated fare via the public Uber/Bolt APIs (or screen‑scrapes the driver app), (c) compares it to the passenger‑charged amount, (d) generates a ready‑to‑send dispute ticket (PDF + pre‑filled email) and stores a log for future reference. Optional push‑notifications warn the user if the driver deviates > 500 m from the pickup point.

**WHO PAYS**  
- Urban commuters (18‑35) who use Uber/Bolt at least twice a month.  
- Small‑business owners who reimburse employee rides.  

**PRICE**  
- Free tier: up to 3 audits per month.  
- Premium: ₦1 200 / month (unlimited audits, auto‑submission to support, priority email help).

**EXISTING COMPETITORS**  
- None that combine GPS logging + automated dispute generation for Nigerian ride‑hailing.  
- Indirect: Uber’s own “Help” chat, third‑party “RideFare” calculators (US‑centric), manual spreadsheet methods.

**MONETIZATION FIT**  
Urban commuters are moderately price‑sensitive but are willing to pay for a tool that saves them from over‑charging or lost time. Small businesses have a clear ROI (reducing reimbursement errors). However, the segment is accustomed to free support channels, so conversion will be modest (~5‑10 % of free users).

**FIRST WEEK**  
1. **Validate** – Run a short survey in Lagos/Abuja university groups (via the marketer) to confirm at least 150 respondents experience fare disputes.  
2. **Prototype** – Build a minimal Android UI that records GPS and captures the Uber receipt screenshot; store locally.  
3. **Landing page** – Publish a one‑page site (static on AWS S3) with a sign‑up form for early‑access; drive traffic with targeted Instagram ads (organic, using the marketer’s accounts).

**WHY NOW**  
Recent Play Store complaints show the problem is fresh; ride‑hailing usage in Nigeria is still growing (≈ 30 % YoY). Uber/Bolt have not released a transparent audit tool for passengers.

**FEASIBILITY SCORES**

| Investment | **9** (only AWS free tier, Play Store account) |
| Passive Income | **5** (requires ongoing support tickets, but premium is subscription) |
| Team Size | **8** (solo‑friendly; 1 dev can ship MVP) |
| Time to Market | **7** (≈ 4 weeks for MVP) |
| Competition | **8** (no direct competitor in Nigeria) |
| Monetization Fit | **5** (moderate willingness to pay) |

## 2. FoodRefund – “Automatic Uber‑Eats / JumiaFood Receipt Audit”

**PROBLEM**  
Users report being charged twice or for items never delivered (Play Store complaints).

**SOLUTION**  
A lightweight Android app that (a) scans or imports the order receipt (PDF, image, or email), (b) parses line‑items using OCR + simple rules, (c) cross‑checks totals against the payment gateway (Paystack/Flutterwave) via API, (d) flags anomalies (duplicate charge, missing item) and offers a one‑tap “Send Refund Request” template to the provider’s support email or chat bot.

**WHO PAYS**  
- Frequent food‑delivery customers (≥ 2 orders/week).  
- Office managers who handle team lunch orders.

**PRICE**  
- Free: up to 5 audits per month.  
- Pro: ₦800 / month (unlimited audits, auto‑submission, multi‑account support).

**EXISTING COMPETITORS**  
- None in Nigeria that automate receipt audit for food‑delivery.  
- Indirect: manual spreadsheet tracking, generic receipt‑scanner apps (e.g., “CamScanner”) that don’t integrate with delivery platforms.

**MONETIZATION FIT**  
Food‑delivery users are price‑sensitive but will pay if they can reliably recover lost money. The average over‑charge per incident is ≈ ₦1 500, so a ₦800/month subscription can be justified after 1‑2 successful refunds.

**FIRST WEEK**  
1. **Collect data** – Ask the marketer to gather 30 real receipts (with permission) from friends who use Uber‑Eats/JumiaFood.  
2. **Build OCR pipeline** – Use Google Vision API (free tier) to extract line items; store locally.  
3. **Create a “demo” video** and post on TikTok/Instagram with a link to a pre‑launch sign‑up page.

**WHY NOW**  
The surge in food‑delivery usage post‑COVID plus the specific complaints about double‑charging indicate a fresh pain point. Providers have weak self‑service refund processes.

**FEASIBILITY SCORES**

## 3. CampusCarpool – “Student‑only Peer‑to‑Peer Ride‑Sharing”

**PROBLEM**  
University students in Nigeria lack affordable, reliable transport between campus, hostels, and city centres; they also distrust commercial ride‑hailing pricing (Play Store complaints about unfair rates).

**SOLUTION**  
A web‑plus‑Android platform where verified university students can post or join rides. Features:  
- Campus‑based authentication (student‑ID upload, email domain verification).  
- Fixed “per‑seat” price set by the driver (transparent, no surge).  
- Integrated mobile‑money payment (Paystack/Flutterwave) that holds escrow until the ride is completed.  
- Rating system limited to the campus community.

**WHO PAYS**  
- Students (pay per seat, typically ₦300‑₦800).  
- University housing offices (could subsidise for safety compliance).

**PRICE**  
- Platform fee: 5 % of each transaction (automatically deducted).  
- Optional premium “Verified Driver” badge: ₦2 000 / year for drivers.

**EXISTING COMPETITORS**  
- General ride‑hailing apps (Uber, Bolt) – not campus‑focused, higher prices.  
- Informal WhatsApp groups (ad‑hoc car‑pools) – no escrow, no rating.  
- No dedicated Nigerian campus‑car‑pool app.

**MONETIZATION FIT**  
Students are highly price‑sensitive, but a small transaction fee is acceptable if the service saves them money vs commercial rides. The 5 % fee is comparable to what existing peer‑to‑peer platforms charge elsewhere.

**FIRST WEEK**  
1. **Partner outreach** – Contact the student union of one university (via the marketer) to get permission to pilot.  
2. **MVP build** – Simple Android app + Firebase backend (free tier) for ride posting, payment integration.  
3. **Beta launch** – Invite 30‑50 students via the union’s mailing list; collect feedback.

**WHY NOW**  
University enrolments in Nigeria are rising (> 2 million new entrants per year). Existing transport options are congested and expensive; a campus‑centric solution meets a clear, growing demand.

**FEASIBILITY SCORES**

| Team Size | **6** (needs both devs for Android & web, plus moderation) |

| Competition | **7** (indirect competition only) |

## 4. TripLoop – “Round‑Trip Public‑Transport Planner”

**PROBLEM**  
Developers (GitHub issue) and everyday commuters need a simple tool to plan “round‑trip” journeys on Nigerian bus/rail networks, which Google Maps often mishandles.

**SOLUTION**  
A web‑app (responsive, also Android WebView) that lets a user enter start, destination, and desired return time. It pulls schedule data from publicly available GTFS feeds (where they exist) and crowdsourced user updates, then outputs the cheapest/fastest round‑trip combination, with an optional “save as PDF” itinerary.

**WHO PAYS**  
- Daily commuters (especially inter‑city bus travellers).  
- Travel agencies that sell round‑trip tickets.

**PRICE**  
- Free tier: basic route lookup (single‑day).  
- Premium: ₦500 / month for “offline PDF export”, “saved favourite routes”, and “real‑time delay alerts”.

**EXISTING COMPETITORS**  
- Google Maps (partial, unreliable for local buses).  
- Local bus‑company websites (static timetables, no round‑trip optimisation).  
- No dedicated round‑trip planner for Nigeria.

**MONETIZATION FIT**  
Commuters are accustomed to free map services, but a niche premium for reliable round‑trip planning (especially for inter‑city travel costing > ₦2 000) can be justified. Adoption will be modest; the biggest revenue may come from travel agencies licensing the API.

**FIRST WEEK**  
1. **Data collection** – Scrape the GTFS feed for Lagos and Abuja (or manually compile schedules).  
2. **Build core algorithm** – Simple graph search for shortest round‑trip given time constraints (Python → Flask on AWS free tier).  
3. **Create a landing page** with a “Try the demo” button; share on university transport forums.

**WHY NOW**  
Increasing inter‑city bus usage and the lack of reliable digital tools create a timing gap. The GitHub issue signals developer interest that can be turned into a product.

**FEASIBILITY SCORES**

## 5. VPNCheck – “Local‑Law VPN Compatibility Scanner”

**PROBLEM**  
Recent legal news (HackerNews) shows users worry that VPNs may become illegal or technically impossible to use (e.g., Utah law). Nigerian users fear similar future restrictions and need a way to verify if a VPN service complies with local regulations.

**SOLUTION**  
A free Android app that (a) runs a set of network tests (DNS leak, IP geolocation, port blocking) against a list of known Nigerian ISP restrictions, (b) cross‑references a curated database of VPN providers’ legal statements, (c) gives a “Compliance Score” (green/yellow/red) and suggests alternatives. The backend (AWS Lambda, free tier) updates the database weekly via community contributions.

**WHO PAYS**  
- Tech‑savvy Nigerians (students, freelancers, remote workers).  
- Small businesses that need secure remote access.

**PRICE**  
- Free core scanner.  
- Pro add‑on: ₦500 / year for “auto‑update notifications” and “enterprise compliance report”.

**EXISTING COMPETITORS**  
- Global VPN review sites (e.g., “VPNMentor”) – not Nigeria‑specific, no automated testing.  
- None that provide a local compliance score.

**MONETIZATION FIT**  
The core audience is accustomed to free security tools, but a modest pro tier for businesses is realistic. Most revenue will come from the enterprise tier; consumer conversion will be low.

**FIRST WEEK**  
1. **Research** – Compile a list of Nigerian ISP blocking behaviours (via public reports).  
2. **Prototype** – Use Android’s VpnService API to run a simple DNS‑leak test; display result.  
3. **Community launch** – Post on r/Nigeria and local tech Discords asking for VPN provider input; seed the database.

**WHY NOW**  
Legal uncertainty around VPNs is rising globally; early‑adopter Nigerians are actively seeking reassurance. The HackerNews story proves the issue is top‑of‑mind for tech‑savvy users.

**FEASIBILITY SCORES**  

| Metric | Score |

| Passive Income | **6** (pro subscriptions, low churn) |
