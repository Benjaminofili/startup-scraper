## 1. WhatsApp CRM for SMEs  

**Problem** – *“customer service is very very very bad, I want to contact to customer past 6 day”* (Play Store reviews). Nigerian small businesses receive most enquiries on WhatsApp but have no ticketing, assignment or analytics tools.  

**Solution** – A lightweight SaaS dashboard that **hooks into the WhatsApp Business API (or Twilio sandbox)**, turns every incoming message into a ticket, lets agents assign, label, and reply from a web UI, and provides basic response‑time analytics. A free tier handles up to 500 messages/month; paid plans unlock multi‑agent seats and deeper reporting.  

**Who pays** – Owners of micro‑retail shops, e‑commerce sellers, service‑providers (beauty salons, mechanics, tutors) who already spend on POS or inventory SaaS.  

**Price** –  
* Free tier – 0 ₦* (≤ 500 msgs/mo)  
* Starter – ₦2 000 / month* (up to 2 agents, 5 k msgs)  
* Growth – ₦5 000 / month* (unlimited msgs, 5 agents, advanced reports)  

**Existing competitors** – Global tools (Zoho Desk, Freshdesk, HubSpot Service) that require a separate WhatsApp number and are priced for larger firms; the native **WhatsApp Business app** (manual, no ticketing). No locally‑tailored, low‑cost SaaS currently dominates the Nigerian market.  

**Monetization fit** – Nigerian SMEs are accustomed to paying modest SaaS fees (e.g., Paystack ₦1 000 / month). They value time‑saving tools, so a ₦2 000 monthly price is realistic, though price‑sensitivity remains high.  

**First‑week launch steps**  

1. **Validate** – interview 5 SME owners (retail, salon, e‑commerce) about their WhatsApp workflow and willingness to pay.  
2. **Prototype** – set up a Twilio WhatsApp sandbox (free tier), build a tiny Node / Express API that logs inbound messages to a DynamoDB table (AWS free tier).  
3. **Pilot** – invite one SME to test the “ticket view” for a week; capture usage data and iterate.  

**Why now** – WhatsApp is the de‑facto customer‑service channel in Nigeria; the recent flood of complaints shows a gap. The Twilio sandbox and AWS free tier let you build a functional MVP without any cash outlay.  

**Feasibility scores** – Investment 9 · Passive 5 · Team 8 · Time 7 · Comp 4 · Monet 6 · Reg 8

## 2. Daycare Connect  

**Problem** – *“daycare”* and *“preschool”* each have > 500 monthly Google‑search volume, yet parents repeatedly complain about lack of reliable information, safety checks, and easy booking.  

**Solution** – A **mobile‑first marketplace** that lists vetted day‑care centres and preschools in major Nigerian cities, shows parent reviews, price ranges, photos, and lets parents **book a trial slot and pay via Paystack**. Providers get a simple admin portal to update availability, accept payments, and reply to enquiries.  

**Who pays** –  
*Parents* (pay‑per‑booking fee or optional subscription for premium alerts).  
*Day‑care operators* (pay for a featured listing or lead‑generation package).  

**Price** –  
* Free listing* – basic profile, no ranking.  
* Featured listing* – ₦5 000 / month (top of search, badge).  
* Booking fee* – ₦1 500 per confirmed enrolment (10 % of centre fee, capped at ₦5 000).  

**Existing competitors** – General classifieds (Jiji, OLX), Facebook groups, informal word‑of‑mouth networks. No dedicated, searchable, review‑driven platform for Nigerian early‑childcare.  

**Monetization fit** – Parents already spend ₦30 000‑₦80 000 per month on childcare; a small convenience fee is acceptable. Day‑care owners are accustomed to paying for leads (e.g., newspaper ads), so a ₦5 000 monthly listing is realistic.  

**First‑week launch steps**  

1. **Survey** – run a 5‑question Google Form in university WhatsApp groups (target 20 parents) to capture pain points and willingness to pay.  
2. **Seed data** – manually collect 30 day‑care listings from existing Facebook pages and Google Maps; store in a simple Firebase Firestore collection (free tier).  
3. **MVP UI** – build a React‑Native list view with filters (city, price, rating) and a “Contact” button that opens WhatsApp with a pre‑filled message.  

**Why now** – Urban middle‑class families are growing fast; the pandemic heightened safety concerns, creating demand for transparent, vetted options. No local competitor has yet built a digital booking flow.  

**Feasibility scores** – Investment 8 · Passive 5 · Team 7 · Time 6 · Comp 6 · Monet 7 · Reg 5

## 3. AgriWeather SMS  

**Problem** – *“weather today”* shows > 500 searches; smallholder farmers lack reliable, **offline** weather alerts, leading to crop loss.  

**Solution** – A **subscription service** that pushes **hyper‑local, SMS/WhatsApp weather forecasts** (rain probability, temperature, pest‑risk alerts) to a farmer’s phone each morning. Uses the free tier of **OpenWeatherMap** (or Meteomatics) and **Twilio SMS** (or local SMS gateway). Farmers can reply “STOP” to unsubscribe.  

**Who pays** – Individual smallholder farmers (subscription) and **agri‑NGOs / cooperatives** that buy bulk licences for their members.  

**Price** –  
* Individual* – ₦500 / month (SMS) or ₦300 / month (WhatsApp).  
* Bulk* – ₦30 000 / month for up to 100 farmers (discounted).  

**Existing competitors** – Generic weather apps (AccuWeather, Weather.com) that require data; **no SMS‑based, Nigeria‑specific service**. Some NGOs run seasonal SMS alerts but they are project‑based, not commercial.  

**Monetization fit** – Farmers have shown willingness to pay for market‑price info (e.g., ₦1 000 / month services). Weather is a high‑value add‑on; NGOs often have grant budgets for farmer outreach, providing a B2B revenue stream.  

**First‑week launch steps**  

1. **Partner** – contact a local agricultural extension office or a farmer‑co‑op to obtain a pilot list of 5‑10 phone numbers.  
2. **Integrate** – set up OpenWeatherMap free API, write a Lambda function that formats a concise SMS and sends via Twilio (free trial credits).  
3. **Pilot** – send the first 3‑day forecast to the pilot group; collect feedback on clarity and usefulness.  

**Why now** – Climate variability is increasing, and mobile‑phone penetration in rural Nigeria is > 80 %. The free‑tier APIs make a fully automated service possible with zero cash outlay.  

**Feasibility scores** – Investment 9 · Passive 7 · Team 8 ·
