After analyzing the problems from real users, I've identified the top 5 startup opportunities for Nigerian university students. Here are the ideas:

**1. RespectMe**
1. **IDEA NAME**: RespectMe
2. **PROBLEM**: Poor customer service from e-commerce platforms and retail stores is a significant pain point for Nigerian consumers.
3. **SOLUTION**: A review and rating platform for Nigerian businesses, allowing customers to share their experiences and rate the quality of service.
4. **WHO PAYS**: Businesses will pay for premium listings and advertising on the platform.
5. **PRICE**: ₦5,000 per month for premium listings.
6. **FIRST WEEK**: 
   - Step 1: Conduct market research to identify the most popular e-commerce platforms and retail stores in Nigeria.
   - Step 2: Develop a basic website and mobile app for customers to leave reviews and ratings.
   - Step 3: Reach out to businesses to offer premium listings and advertising opportunities.
7. **WHY NOW**: The complaint on the PlayStore about poor customer service highlights the need for a platform that holds businesses accountable for their customer service.
8. **FEASIBILITY SCORES**:
   - Investment: 8/10 (initial development costs, but potential for passive income)
   - Passive Income: 6/10 (some manual work required for moderation and customer support)
   - Team Size: 7/10 (can be managed with a small team, but may require additional staff as the platform grows)
   - Time to Market: 8/10 (can be launched within a few weeks with a basic version)

**2. DurableNG**
1. **IDEA NAME**: DurableNG
2. **PROBLEM**: Nigerian consumers struggle to find reliable and durable products, leading to wasted money and frustration.
3. **SOLUTION**: A Rotten Tomatoes-style platform for durable products, allowing customers to rate and review products based on their durability and performance.
4. **WHO PAYS**: Manufacturers and retailers will pay for premium listings and advertising on the platform.
5. **PRICE**: ₦3,000 per month for premium listings.
6. **FIRST WEEK**: 
   - Step 1: Research popular product categories in Nigeria and identify the most durable products.
   - Step 2: Develop a basic website and mobile app for customers to leave reviews and ratings.
   - Step 3: Reach out to manufacturers and retailers to offer premium listings and advertising opportunities.
7. **WHY NOW**: The Show HN post about a Rotten Tomatoes-style platform for durable products highlights the potential for a similar platform in Nigeria.
8. **FEASIBILITY SCORES**:
   - Investment: 8/10 (initial development costs, but potential for passive income)
   - Passive Income: 7/10 (some manual work required for moderation and customer support)
   - Team Size: 7/10 (can be managed with a small team, but may require additional staff as the platform grows)
   - Time to Market: 8/10 (can be launched within a few weeks with a basic version)

**3. LibreTaxiNG**
1. **IDEA NAME**: LibreTaxiNG
2. **PROBLEM**: Nigerian consumers struggle with high transportation costs and poor service from traditional taxi companies.
3. **SOLUTION**: A free and open-source alternative to Uber and Lyft, allowing customers to book rides with independent drivers.
4. **WHO PAYS**: Drivers will pay a commission on each ride, and customers will pay for rides.
5. **PRICE**: 10% commission on each ride.
6. **FIRST WEEK**: 
   - Step 1: Research the Nigerian transportation market and identify potential partners (e.g., driver associations).
   - Step 2: Develop a basic website and mobile app for customers to book rides.
   - Step 3: Recruit drivers and launch a pilot program in a major city.
7. **WHY NOW**: The LibreTaxi post on HackerNews highlights the potential for a free and open-source alternative to traditional taxi companies.
8. **FEASIBILITY SCORES**:
   - Investment: 6/10 (initial development costs, and potential for significant investment in marketing and driver recruitment)
   - Passive Income: 5/10 (significant manual work required for driver recruitment, customer support, and moderation)
   - Team Size: 5/10 (requires a larger team to manage driver recruitment, customer support, and marketing)
   - Time to Market: 6/10 (may take several months to launch a fully functional platform)

**4. NotionNG**

7. **WHY NOW**: The Docs post on HackerNews highlights the potential for an open-source alternative to Notion.
8. **FEASIBILITY SCORES**:

   - Passive Income: 8/10 (potential for donations or sponsored features)
   - Team Size: 8/10 (can be managed with a small team, and potential for community contributions)
   - Time to Market: 9/10 (can be launched within a few weeks with a basic version)

**5. FocusApp**
1. **IDEA NAME**: FocusApp
2. **PROBLEM**: Nigerian students and professionals struggle with distractions and procrastination, leading to decreased productivity.
3. **SOLUTION**: An app that helps users stay focused by blocking distracting websites and apps, and providing productivity tracking features.
4. **WHO PAYS**: Users will pay a subscription fee for premium features.
5. **PRICE**: ₦1,000 per month for premium features.
6. **FIRST WEEK**:

7. **WHY NOW**: The Show HN post about an app to stop doomscrolling highlights the potential for a focus-enhancing app.

   - Passive Income: 7/10 (some manual work required for customer support and updates)

   - Time to Market: 8/10 (can be launched within a few weeks with a basic version)
